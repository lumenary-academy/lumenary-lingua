<Section id="key-forms" kind="vocabulary" title="Periphrases and two-faced adjectives">

By now you can conjugate every tense in both moods (see <LessonLink slug="feelings-and-doubt">Feelings and doubt</LessonLink> and <LessonLink slug="if-i-were">If I were</LessonLink>). This lesson is about the choices that remain once the forms are under control: when both moods are grammatical, which one says what you mean; how Spanish expresses *aspect* — whether an action is beginning, repeated, ongoing, just finished — with small verb chains called **perífrasis verbales**; and how one adjective can mean two things depending on whether it follows *ser* or *estar*.

<Section id="two-moods" kind="grammar" title="One conjunction, two moods">

You learned at B1 that the subjunctive follows wishes, doubts and emotions. At C1 a more useful rule of thumb is this: **the indicative asserts; the subjunctive does not.** A subjunctive clause may be false, uncertain, hypothetical — or perfectly true but *not the point*, information the speaker takes as known and does not put forward as news. That last case explains most of the pairs below.

### *Aunque*

| | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| *Aunque **llueve**, vamos a salir.* | It *is* raining (I'm telling you), but we're going out. |
| *Aunque **llueva**, vamos a salir.* | (a) Even if it rains… (b) Even though it's raining — we both know, and it doesn't matter. |

Reading (b) is the one English speakers miss. Listen to this exchange:

> —Es tu jefe, no puedes contestarle así.
> —**Aunque sea** mi jefe, no tiene derecho a gritarme.

The speaker knows perfectly well he is her boss: the subjunctive concedes the point as already established and irrelevant. *Aunque es mi jefe* would sound as if she were announcing it.

### *El hecho de que*

*El hecho de que* normally takes the **subjunctive**, even though it introduces a fact, because the fact is the topic under discussion rather than new information:

- ***El hecho de que no haya llamado** me preocupa.* — That she hasn't called worries me.

The indicative also occurs, especially in speech and when the clause comes after the verb as news (*Me preocupa el hecho de que no ha llamado*). Default to the subjunctive in writing. Note that *el hecho es que* is different: it asserts, so it takes the indicative — ***El hecho es que** no ha llamado.*

### *Como*

At the start of a sentence, *como* + indicative gives a **cause**; *como* + subjunctive gives a **condition**, usually a warning or threat — a construction especially common in Spain:

- ***Como no viniste**, me fui.* — Since you didn't come, I left.
- ***Como no vengas**, me enfado.* — If you don't come, I'll be cross.

As a word of manner, *como* + indicative refers to a known way, + subjunctive to any way at all: *Lo hice **como querías*** (the way you wanted) vs *Hazlo **como quieras*** (however you like).

### *De ahí que*

As you saw in <LessonLink slug="argument-and-connectors">Building an argument</LessonLink>, *de ahí que* introduces a consequence the reader can already see, and it takes the subjunctive: *No había estudiado; **de ahí que suspendiera**.* The indicative is occasionally found, but careful writing keeps the subjunctive.

### Verbs that change meaning with mood

Some main verbs have two senses, and the mood of the clause after them tells you which:

| Verb | + indicative | + subjunctive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *decir que* | report: *Dice que **viene**.* (He says he's coming.) | order: *Dice que **vengas**.* (He says you should come.) |
| *sentir que* | perceive: *Siento que **hace** frío.* (I feel it's cold.) | regret: *Siento que **te vayas**.* (I'm sorry you're leaving.) |
| *comprender que* | realise: *Comprendo que **estás** cansada.* (I see you're tired.) | sympathise: *Comprendo que **estés** cansada.* (It's understandable that you're tired.) |
| *insistir en que* | maintain: *Insiste en que **es** inocente.* | demand: *Insiste en que **firmemos**.* |
| *temer(se) que* | polite "I'm afraid": *Me temo que no **quedan** entradas.* | real fear: *Temo que no **queden** entradas.* |

### *Quizá*, *tal vez*, *probablemente*

After these adverbs both moods are correct, and the choice is a shade of confidence: *Quizá **tiene** razón* leans towards "she's probably right"; *Quizá **tenga** razón* leaves more room for doubt. *A lo mejor*, by contrast, always takes the indicative: *A lo mejor **tiene** razón.*

</Section>

<Section id="periphrases" kind="grammar" title="Verbal periphrases: aspect in a verb chain">

A periphrasis is a conjugated auxiliary followed by an infinitive, a gerund or a participle, working as a single unit of meaning. English does much of this with adverbs (*just*, *again*, *still*, *gradually*); Spanish builds it into the verb.

### Duration up to now

***Llevar*** + time + gerund is the natural way to say "I've been doing it for…": *Llevo tres años estudiando chino.* It is more idiomatic than *Hace tres años que estudio chino*, and it never uses the present perfect continuous English speakers reach for (*he estado estudiando* is possible but heavier). The negative uses *sin* + infinitive: *Llevamos una semana **sin** salir.*

***Venir*** + gerund also covers a stretch of time up to now, often with a note of insistence or complaint: *Te lo **vengo diciendo** desde enero* — I've been telling you since January (and you haven't listened).

### Beginning, interrupting, repeating

- ***Ponerse a*** + infinitive marks a start, deliberate or sudden: *Se sentó y **se puso a** escribir.* *De repente **se puso a** granizar.*
- ***Echarse a*** + infinitive is "to burst into", with a short list of verbs: *reír, llorar, correr, temblar*. *Romper a* works with *llorar* and *reír* too.
- ***Dejar de*** + infinitive stops an action: *Ha dejado de nevar.* In the negative imperative it becomes an emphatic recommendation: ***No dejes de** probar el ceviche* — be sure to try the ceviche.
- ***Volver a*** + infinitive repeats: *No **volveré a** confiar en él.* It replaces "again" (*otra vez*) and is more elegant.

### Just, still, gradually

- ***Acabar de*** + infinitive in the present or imperfect means "have / had just": *Acabo de llegar.* *Acababa de salir cuando sonó el teléfono.* In other tenses it often simply means "finish": *Cuando **acabe de** comer, te llamo.* In the negative it means "not quite": *No **acabo de** entender su postura* — I don't quite follow her position.
- ***Seguir*** + gerund means "still": *¿**Sigues** trabajando en el banco?* With *sin*: *Sigue **sin** contestar* — she still hasn't answered.
- ***Ir*** + gerund describes gradual progress: *Poco a poco **voy** acostumbrándome.*
- ***Andar*** + gerund means going around doing something, often with disapproval: ***Anda** diciendo que le debo dinero.*

### Results

***Tener*** + participle stresses the accumulated result, and the participle **agrees** with the object: *Tengo **escritas** tres páginas.* *Ya tenemos **vendidas** todas las entradas.* Compare the neutral *He escrito tres páginas*.

</Section>

<Section id="ser-estar" kind="grammar" title="Adjectives that change meaning with ser and estar">

With most adjectives, *ser* classifies (what kind of thing it is) and *estar* describes a state or a perception (how it is, or seems, now). For a group of adjectives the difference is so large that they become different words.

| Adjective | with *ser* | with *estar* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *listo* | *Tu hija **es** muy lista.* — clever | *¿**Estás** listo?* — ready |
| *rico* | *Su familia **es** rica.* — wealthy | *La sopa **está** rica.* — tasty |
| *malo* | *Esa película **es** mala.* — bad | *Hoy **estoy** malo.* — ill (especially in Spain) |
| *verde* | *La puerta **es** verde.* — green | *Los plátanos **están** verdes.* — unripe; *Todavía **está** verde en esto.* — inexperienced |
| *vivo* | *Es un niño muy **vivo**.* — sharp, lively | *El pez todavía **está vivo**.* — alive |
| *despierto* | ***Es** muy despierta.* — bright | *¿**Estás** despierta?* — awake |
| *orgulloso* | ***Es** muy orgulloso.* — haughty | ***Estoy** orgullosa de ti.* — proud of you |
| *atento* | ***Es** muy atento con sus clientes.* — considerate | ***Está** atento a la pizarra.* — paying attention |
| *aburrido* | *La clase **es** aburrida.* — boring | ***Estoy** aburrido.* — bored |
| *interesado* | *No me fío: **es** muy interesado.* — self-serving | ***Está** interesada en el puesto.* — interested |
| *delicado* | *Es un tema **delicado**.* — sensitive, tactful | *Mi abuelo **está** delicado.* — frail |

Two more refinements:

- **The *estar* of impression.** With adjectives that normally go with *ser*, *estar* turns a classification into a perception: *¡Qué joven **estás**!* — you look so young. *El niño **está** muy alto* — he's got so tall.
- ***Bueno* and *malo* with people.** *Es bueno* means he is a good person; *está bueno* describes food as tasty — but said of a person, colloquially, it means "attractive" in a physical sense. Keep it out of the office.

</Section>

<Section id="reading-carta" kind="reading" title="Reading: «La carta que no acababa de escribir»">

This short story was written for this lesson. Read it once for the plot, then again for the forms in bold.

> **La carta que no acababa de escribir**
>
> Itziar **lleva tres semanas intentando** escribirle a su hermano. Se sienta a la mesa de la cocina, **se pone a escribir** con energía y, al cabo de dos líneas, **deja de hacerlo**. No es que no **tenga** nada que decirle; es que tiene demasiado.
>
> Mikel vive en Montevideo desde hace once años y, aunque hablan por teléfono cada domingo, ella siente que algo **se les ha ido perdiendo** por el camino. Las llamadas son agradables, eso sí: él **anda contando** siempre las mismas anécdotas del trabajo, ella le pregunta por los niños, y los dos **se echan a reír** con los recuerdos de la infancia. Pero las cosas importantes se quedan sin decir.
>
> Esta noche **ha vuelto a intentarlo**. **Tiene escritas** ya dos páginas, lo cual es un récord. Le cuenta que su madre **está** mayor y algo **delicada**, aunque **sigue siendo** la persona más **despierta** de la familia; que el sobrino, que **acaba de cumplir** dieciocho años, **está verde** todavía para casi todo, pero **es** un chico **listo** y muy **atento** con su abuela; que ella misma **está orgullosa** de haber cambiado de trabajo, aunque a veces **se sienta** perdida.
>
> **Como Mikel no conteste** a esta carta, piensa, me voy a enfadar de verdad. Luego se ríe de sí misma: **el hecho de que** su hermano **tarde** en contestar nunca ha significado que no la **quiera**. **Viene diciéndoselo** a sí misma desde hace años y, poco a poco, **se lo va creyendo**.
>
> A medianoche, por fin, la carta **está lista**. La relee, cambia dos palabras y escribe la última frase: «**Aunque estemos** lejos, sigues siendo mi hermano pequeño. **No dejes de venir** este verano». Luego dobla las hojas, las mete en un sobre y **se pone a buscar** un sello, que es lo más difícil que ha hecho en todo el día.

| Form | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| *lleva tres semanas intentando* | duration up to now (*llevar* + gerund) |
| *se les ha ido perdiendo* | a gradual loss (*ir* + gerund), in the present perfect |
| *anda contando* | habit, with a hint of gentle mockery |
| *tiene escritas dos páginas* | accumulated result; *escritas* agrees with *páginas* |
| *aunque a veces se sienta perdida* | a known fact, conceded and set aside: subjunctive |
| *Como Mikel no conteste* | *como* + subjunctive: a (mock) threat |
| *el hecho de que su hermano tarde* | the fact as topic: subjunctive |
| *Aunque estemos lejos* | they *are* far apart; the subjunctive says "and it doesn't matter" |
| *No dejes de venir* | "be sure to come" |

Notice also the adjectives: *está delicada* (frail), *es despierta* (sharp-minded), *está verde* (inexperienced), *es listo* (clever), *está lista* (ready).

</Section>

<Section id="regional-aspect" kind="culture" title="Periphrases across the Spanish-speaking world">

Periphrases are where regional Spanish shows some of its most interesting differences.

- **Andar + gerundio** is everyday and neutral in Mexico and Central America: *¿Qué andas haciendo?* simply means "What are you up to?" In Spain it more often carries the disapproving tone described above.
- **Estar por + infinitivo** means "to be about to" in much of Latin America (*El tren está por salir*), but in Spain it usually means "to be inclined to": *Estoy por no ir* — I'm half-minded not to go.
- **Recién** with a conjugated verb means "just" in much of Latin America, especially the Southern Cone: *Recién llego* or *Recién llegué* — I've just arrived. In Spain *recién* goes only before participles: *recién llegado*, *recién pintado*.
- **Ir a + infinitivo** has largely replaced the simple future in everyday speech across Latin America (*Mañana voy a llamarte*), and is gaining ground in Spain too. The simple future survives in writing and for probability: *¿Qué hora será?*

None of these is more correct than another. What matters at C1 is that you recognise them, and that when you choose one you know what your listener will hear.

</Section>

<Section id="misconceptions" kind="misconceptions" title="Common misconceptions">

<Misconception>

**"The subjunctive means doubt."** Often it does, but *Aunque sea mi jefe*, *el hecho de que no haya llamado* and *de ahí que suspendiera* all refer to facts the speaker knows are true. The better rule: the subjunctive marks information that is *not being asserted*, whether because it is uncertain or because it is already taken for granted.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"*Acabar de* always means 'just'."** Only in the present and imperfect, and only with a time reading. *Cuando acabe de leer, te lo presto* means "when I finish reading". *No acabo de verlo claro* means "I don't quite see it clearly".

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"*Ser* is permanent, *estar* is temporary."** A useful first approximation that breaks quickly: *está muerto* is as permanent as it gets, and *es joven* will not last. Think instead: *ser* classifies and identifies; *estar* describes a state, a result or how something seems.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"*Venir de* + infinitive means 'to have just'."** That is French (*venir de faire*). In Spanish, *Vengo de comprar pan* means "I'm coming back from buying bread". For "I've just bought bread" say *Acabo de comprar pan*.

</Misconception>

</Section>
