<Section id="intro" kind="motivation" title="Telling what happened">

"What did you do at the weekend?" is one of the most common questions in any language, and until now you couldn't answer it in Spanish. This lesson introduces the **preterite** (*el pretérito indefinido* or *pretérito perfecto simple*), the tense for completed actions in the past: *I travelled*, *we ate*, *they arrived*. Regular verbs follow a clear pattern, and the few spelling changes all have one simple reason behind them. Irregular verbs like *ir*, *ser* and *hacer* come in the <LessonLink slug="trips-and-irregular-pasts">next lesson</LessonLink>.

</Section>

<Section id="preterite" kind="grammar" title="The preterite of regular verbs">

Take the stem (*habl-, com-, viv-*) and add these endings:

| | hablar | comer | vivir |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| yo | habl**é** | com**í** | viv**í** |
| tú | habl**aste** | com**iste** | viv**iste** |
| él, ella, usted | habl**ó** | com**ió** | viv**ió** |
| nosotros, nosotras | habl**amos** | com**imos** | viv**imos** |
| vosotros, vosotras | habl**asteis** | com**isteis** | viv**isteis** |
| ellos, ellas, ustedes | habl**aron** | com**ieron** | viv**ieron** |

Three things to notice:

1. **-er and -ir verbs share one set of endings** in the preterite, all with *i*.
2. **The yo and él forms carry a written accent**, and it matters: *hablo* is "I speak", *habló* is "he spoke". *Trabajo* is "I work"; *trabajó*, "she worked".
3. **The nosotros forms of -ar and -ir verbs are the same as in the present**: *hablamos* can be "we speak" or "we spoke", *vivimos* "we live" or "we lived". Context, usually a time word, makes it clear: <Say lang="es">Ayer hablamos con Ana.</Say> -er verbs are different: *comemos* (present), *comimos* (preterite).

Some examples:

- <Say lang="es">Ayer trabajé diez horas.</Say> Yesterday I worked ten hours.
- <Say lang="es">¿Cenaste en casa anoche?</Say> Did you have dinner at home last night?
- <Say lang="es">Mi hermano vivió dos años en Quito.</Say> My brother lived in Quito for two years.
- <Say lang="es">Bebimos agua de coco en la playa.</Say> We drank coconut water on the beach.
- <Say lang="es">Mis primos salieron a las once.</Say> My cousins went out at eleven.

**Reflexive verbs** keep their pronouns: <Say lang="es">Me levanté tarde.</Say> <Say lang="es">Nos quedamos en casa.</Say> **Gustar** and **encantar** are regular too: <Say lang="es">Me gustó la película.</Say> <Say lang="es">Nos encantaron los museos.</Say>

**Ver** is regular but needs no accents, because its forms have one syllable: <Say lang="es">vi</Say>, <Say lang="es">viste</Say>, <Say lang="es">vio</Say>, <Say lang="es">vimos</Say>, <Say lang="es">visteis</Say>, <Say lang="es">vieron</Say>. *Anoche vi una película.*

</Section>

<Section id="spelling" kind="grammar" title="Spelling changes that keep the sound">

The *yo* ending *-é* puts an *e* right after the last consonant of the stem, and that can change how the consonant sounds (remember from lesson 1: *c* before *e* is *th* or *s*, *g* before *e* is like *j*). So the spelling changes to **keep the original sound**:

| Infinitive ends in | yo form | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| -car | -**qué** | *buscar → busqué*, *tocar → toqué*, *practicar → practiqué* |
| -gar | -**gué** | *llegar → llegué*, *pagar → pagué*, *jugar → jugué* |
| -zar | -**cé** | *empezar → empecé*, *almorzar → almorcé*, *cruzar → crucé* |

Only the *yo* form changes; the others are regular: *buscaste, buscó, buscamos…*; *llegaste, llegó…*

**i becomes y.** When an -er or -ir stem ends in a vowel (*le-er*, *cre-er*, *o-ír*), the endings *-ió* and *-ieron* would put three vowels in a row, so the *i* becomes *y*: <Say lang="es">leyó</Say>, <Say lang="es">leyeron</Say>; *creyó, creyeron* (believed); *oyó, oyeron* (heard). The other forms gain accents: *leí, leíste, leímos, leísteis*.

### What happens to stem-changing verbs?

- **-ar and -er stem-changers are regular in the preterite**: *pensé, volví, empecé, me desperté, me acosté, perdimos*. No *ie* or *ue* at all.
- **-ir stem-changers change only in the él and ellos forms**, and only by one step: **e → i** and **o → u**. <Say lang="es">pidió</Say>, <Say lang="es">pidieron</Say>; <Say lang="es">se vistió</Say>; <Say lang="es">durmió</Say>, <Say lang="es">durmieron</Say>. The *yo* form is regular: *pedí, dormí*.

</Section>

<Section id="markers" kind="grammar" title="When? Time markers and the personal a">

The preterite goes naturally with expressions of finished time:

- <Say lang="es">ayer</Say>, <Say lang="es">anteayer</Say>, <Say lang="es">anoche</Say>
- <Say lang="es">el sábado</Say> (last Saturday, in a past context), <Say lang="es">el verano pasado</Say> (last summer), <Say lang="es">la semana pasada</Say>
- <Say lang="es">hace tres días</Say> (three days ago), <Say lang="es">hace un año</Say>: *hace* + period = "ago"
- <Say lang="es">en 2015</Say>, <Say lang="es">en agosto</Say>
- to tell a sequence: <Say lang="es">primero</Say>, <Say lang="es">luego</Say>, <Say lang="es">después</Say>, <Say lang="es">al final</Say> (in the end)

<Note>

**Spain and "today".** For things that happened earlier today, this week or this year, speakers in most of Spain prefer another past tense, the present perfect: *Hoy he comido con mi madre*. In Latin America, and in parts of Spain such as the Canary Islands and Galicia, the preterite is used: *Hoy comí con mi madre*. You'll learn the present perfect in level B1; for now, the preterite is understood everywhere.

</Note>

**The personal a.** When the direct object of a verb is a specific person (or a pet you think of as one), Spanish puts **a** before it. It has no English translation:

- <Say lang="es">Visité a mis abuelos.</Say> I visited my grandparents.
- <Say lang="es">Conocí a Marta en una fiesta.</Say> I met Marta at a party.
- <Say lang="es">Vimos a Diego en el parque.</Say> We saw Diego in the park.

But: <Say lang="es">Visité el museo.</Say> <Say lang="es">Vimos una película.</Say> No *a* before things. And *a* + *el* still contracts: <Say lang="es">Vi al profesor.</Say>

</Section>

<Section id="dialogue" kind="dialogue" title="Monday morning in Lima">

Rosa and Kenji work at the same office in Lima, Peru. Kenji's grandparents came to Peru from Japan; Rosa is from Arequipa. It's Monday morning.

| | Spanish | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kenji | <Say lang="es">¡Hola, Rosa! ¿Qué tal el fin de semana?</Say> | Hi, Rosa! How was the weekend? |
| Rosa | <Say lang="es">¡Muy bien! El sábado viajé a Paracas con mi familia.</Say> | Very good! On Saturday I travelled to Paracas with my family. |
| Kenji | <Say lang="es">¡Qué bueno! ¿Nadaron en el mar?</Say> | Great! Did you swim in the sea? |
| Rosa | <Say lang="es">Sí, nadamos, comimos pescado y por la noche paseamos por la playa. Mis hijos jugaron en la arena todo el día.</Say> | Yes, we swam, ate fish and in the evening we walked along the beach. My children played in the sand all day. |
| Kenji | <Say lang="es">¿Y viste los pingüinos?</Say> | And did you see the penguins? |
| Rosa | <Say lang="es">Sí. El domingo tomamos un barco a las islas Ballestas y vimos pingüinos y lobos marinos. ¡Me encantó! ¿Y tú?</Say> | Yes. On Sunday we took a boat to the Ballestas Islands and saw penguins and sea lions. I loved it! And you? |
| Kenji | <Say lang="es">Yo me quedé en Lima. El sábado limpié el departamento y por la tarde jugué al fútbol con unos amigos.</Say> | I stayed in Lima. On Saturday I cleaned the flat and in the afternoon I played football with some friends. |
| Rosa | <Say lang="es">¿Ganaron?</Say> | Did you win? |
| Kenji | <Say lang="es">No, perdimos cinco a dos. Pero después cenamos juntos en un chifa.</Say> | No, we lost five–two. But afterwards we had dinner together at a chifa. |
| Rosa | <Say lang="es">¿Y ayer?</Say> | And yesterday? |
| Kenji | <Say lang="es">Visité a mis abuelos y anoche vi una película con mi hermana. Llegué a casa muy tarde.</Say> | I visited my grandparents, and last night I watched a film with my sister. I got home very late. |
| Rosa | <Say lang="es">Entonces necesitas un café. ¡Te invito!</Say> | Then you need a coffee. My treat! |

**Notes.** *¡Qué bueno!* (great, how nice) is very common in Latin America. *Ustedes* in *¿Nadaron?* is the Latin American plural "you"; in Spain, *¿Nadasteis?* *El departamento* is a flat or apartment in Peru, Mexico and Argentina (*el piso* in Spain). *Un chifa* is a Chinese-Peruvian restaurant, part of Peru's mixed food heritage. *¡Te invito!* (I'm inviting you) means "it's on me".

</Section>

<Section id="pronunciation" kind="pronunciation" title="One accent, two tenses">

The written accent on the preterite *yo* and *él* forms marks a real difference in stress, and with it a difference in meaning. Practise these pairs, stressing the capitalised syllable:

| Present (I…) | Preterite (he, she, you…) |
| --- | --- |
| <Say lang="es">hablo</Say> HA-blo, I speak | <Say lang="es">habló</Say> ha-BLÓ, he spoke |
| <Say lang="es">llego</Say> LLE-go, I arrive | <Say lang="es">llegó</Say> lle-GÓ, she arrived |
| <Say lang="es">trabajo</Say> tra-BA-jo, I work | <Say lang="es">trabajó</Say> tra-ba-JÓ, he worked |
| <Say lang="es">canto</Say> CAN-to, I sing | <Say lang="es">cantó</Say> can-TÓ, she sang |

The spelling changes don't change the sound: *busqué* rhymes with *tomé*, and *llegué* with *bailé*. In *gué* the *u* is silent, just as in *guitarra*.

</Section>

<Section id="culture" kind="culture" title="Puentes and long weekends">

Public holidays shape the Spanish-speaking year: Christmas and Holy Week (*Semana Santa*) everywhere, plus national days such as Peru's *Fiestas Patrias* at the end of July and Mexico's Independence Day on 16 September, and many local saints' days. When a holiday falls on a Tuesday or a Thursday, many people in Spain and Latin America take the Monday or Friday off too and make a *puente* ("bridge"): a four-day weekend. *¿Qué tal el puente?* (How was the long weekend?) is a typical Monday question. In some countries, including Colombia and Argentina, certain holidays are moved to a Monday by law to create long weekends.

In Spain, August is the traditional month for summer holidays, and some small shops and businesses close for part of it. In the southern hemisphere, in Chile, Argentina or Uruguay, the long summer holidays fall in January and February.

</Section>

<Section id="misconceptions" kind="misconceptions" title="Common mix-ups">

<Misconception>

"The accent on *habló* is optional." It isn't: without it, *hablo* means "I speak". In the preterite, the *yo* and *él/ella/usted* forms of regular verbs need their accents.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

"*Buscé* and *llegé*." The *yo* forms change spelling to keep the sound of the consonant: **busqué**, **llegué**, and **empecé** (*z* becomes *c* before *e*).

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

"*Visité mis abuelos.*" When the direct object is a specific person, Spanish adds the personal *a*: **Visité a mis abuelos**.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

"*Vió* and *ví* need accents like *comió* and *comí*." One-syllable forms take no accent: **vi, vio**. (Old texts may show them with accents; the current rules don't.)

</Misconception>

</Section>
