<Section id="vocabulary" kind="vocabulary" title="The language of letters and emails">

Sooner or later you will write to a landlord, a university, a company or a public office in Spanish. A formal letter follows conventions that are easy to learn and, once learned, make your Spanish look far more confident than it may feel. This lesson gives you those conventions, the grammar of *usted*, and two model texts to adapt.

<Section id="usted" kind="grammar" title="Usted and ustedes, consistently">

Formal Spanish addresses the reader as **usted** (one person) or **ustedes** (several people, or a company). Grammatically, *usted* is a third person: the verb, the pronouns and the possessives all follow suit. The commonest error in learners' letters is to slip back into *tú* halfway through.

| | Informal (*tú*) | Formal (*usted*) | Formal plural (*ustedes*) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Verb | *¿Puedes…?* | *¿Puede…?* | *¿Pueden…?* |
| Object pronoun | *te* envío | *le* envío, *se lo* envío | *les* envío, *se lo* envío |
| Possessive | *tu* respuesta | *su* respuesta | *su* respuesta |
| After a preposition | *para ti* | *para usted* | *para ustedes* |
| Command | *Envíame…* | *Envíeme…* | *Envíenme…* |

Because *su* can mean "his, her, its, their, your", formal letters sometimes add *de usted* for clarity: *la dirección de usted*. In writing, *usted* and *ustedes* are often abbreviated **Ud.** and **Uds.** (in Spain also *Vd.*, *Vds.*), but writing them in full is more courteous.

### Polite structures

Four patterns do most of the work in a formal request:

- <Say lang="es">Le agradecería que me enviara la factura.</Say> — I'd be grateful if you sent me the invoice. (Conditional + *que* + imperfect subjunctive, from <LessonLink slug="if-i-were">If I were…</LessonLink>.)
- <Say lang="es">Le ruego que me confirme la fecha.</Say> — Please confirm the date. (*Rogar*: present + present subjunctive.)
- <Say lang="es">¿Sería tan amable de indicarme el horario?</Say> — Would you be so kind as to tell me the opening hours?
- <Say lang="es">Quisiera solicitar información sobre el curso.</Say> — I would like to request information about the course.

A formula you will see in notices: *Les rogamos disculpen las molestias* (We apologise for any inconvenience). In this fixed phrase, the *que* is traditionally left out.

### Register: choosing the formal word

Spanish has many pairs of everyday and formal verbs. In a formal letter, prefer the right-hand column:

| Everyday | Formal | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *pedir* | *solicitar* | *Solicito una cita.* |
| *mandar* | *enviar, remitir* | *Le remito el formulario.* |
| *decir* | *comunicar, informar, indicar* | *Le comunico que…* |
| *dar* | *proporcionar, facilitar* | *¿Podría facilitarme sus datos?* |
| *arreglar* | *solucionar, resolver* | *…para resolver este problema* |
| *empezar* | *comenzar, iniciar* | *El plazo se inicia el lunes.* |
| *mirar, ver* | *comprobar, observar* | *Comprobé que estaba roto.* |
| *un montón de* | *numerosos, una gran cantidad de* | *en numerosas ocasiones* |

<Example title="From a text message to a formal email">

A message to a friend: *Hola, ¿me puedes mandar la factura? La necesito para el viernes. ¡Gracias!*

The same request to an accountant you have never met:

<Say lang="es">Estimada señora Ruiz: Le escribo para solicitarle la factura correspondiente a mi pedido. Le agradecería que me la enviara antes del viernes, ya que la necesito para un trámite. Quedo a la espera de su respuesta. Atentamente, Pablo Méndez</Say>

What changed: *Hola* became *Estimada señora Ruiz:* with a colon; every *tú* form became *usted* (*¿me puedes mandar?* → *le agradecería que me la enviara*); *mandar* became *enviar*, and the implied *pedir* became *solicitar*; a reason was added with a formal connector (*ya que*); and the message gained a closing formula and a sign-off. On the page, the greeting, each paragraph and the sign-off would go on separate lines.

</Example>

</Section>

<Section id="connectors" kind="grammar" title="Formal connectors and structure">

### Connectors

| Function | Everyday | Formal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adding | *además, también* | *asimismo, por otra parte, igualmente* |
| Contrasting | *pero* | *sin embargo, no obstante* |
| Conceding | *aunque, a pesar de* | *si bien, pese a* |
| Consequence | *así que, por eso* | *por consiguiente, por lo tanto, en consecuencia* |
| Cause | *porque* | *dado que, puesto que, ya que* |
| Topic | *sobre* | *en cuanto a, con respecto a, en relación con* |
| Sequence | *primero, luego* | *en primer lugar, a continuación, por último* |
| Concluding | *para terminar* | *en conclusión, por todo lo expuesto* |

### The shape of a formal letter or email

1. **Place and date** (letters): *Madrid, 19 de noviembre de 2026*. Months and days of the week are written in lower case.
2. **Subject** (emails): *Asunto: Solicitud de información sobre el máster*.
3. **Greeting, followed by a colon**: *Estimada señora López:*
4. **Opening**, saying why you are writing: *Me dirijo a usted para…, Le escribo con el fin de…*
5. **Body**: the facts, in order, with connectors.
6. **Request**: *Le agradecería que…, Le ruego que…*
7. **Closing formula**: *Quedo a la espera de su respuesta.*
8. **Sign-off**, followed by a comma, then your **name**: *Atentamente,*

</Section>

<Section id="reading" kind="reading" title="Reading: a complaint and a cover letter">

**Two model texts, written for this lesson.** The first is a complaint to an online shop in Spain; the second, a cover letter for a job at a science museum in Colombia. Both are fictional.

**1. Una reclamación**

> <Say lang="es">Asunto: Reclamación por el pedido n.º 48213</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Estimados señores:</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Me dirijo a ustedes para presentar una reclamación sobre el pedido n.º 48213, realizado en su tienda en línea el 3 de octubre.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">El pedido, una cafetera de 120 euros, debía entregarse en un plazo de cinco días laborables. Sin embargo, no llegó hasta el 21 de octubre y, al abrir la caja, comprobé que el depósito de agua estaba roto. Adjunto dos fotografías en las que puede observarse el daño.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Ese mismo día me puse en contacto con su servicio de atención al cliente, donde se me aseguró que recibiría una respuesta en un plazo de 48 horas. Desde entonces han pasado tres semanas y no he recibido ninguna comunicación.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Por consiguiente, les ruego que procedan al reembolso del importe total en un plazo máximo de diez días o, en su defecto, que me envíen un aparato nuevo sin coste adicional.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Quedo a la espera de su respuesta.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Atentamente,</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Carmen Llorente Díaz</Say>

**2. Una carta de presentación**

> <Say lang="es">Estimada señora Vargas:</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Por medio de la presente, quisiera presentar mi candidatura al puesto de guía educativo publicado en su página web el pasado 2 de noviembre.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Soy biólogo y, durante los últimos tres años, he trabajado como monitor en programas de educación ambiental para colegios. Esta experiencia me ha permitido comunicarme con públicos muy diversos, desde niños de seis años hasta grupos de docentes.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Me interesa especialmente su museo por su enfoque práctico de la ciencia, que coincide con mi forma de entender la divulgación. Asimismo, hablo inglés con fluidez y tengo un nivel intermedio de portugués, lo que podría resultar útil en las visitas de grupos internacionales.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Le adjunto mi hoja de vida y quedo a su disposición para ampliar cualquier información en una entrevista.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Le agradezco de antemano su atención.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Cordialmente,</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Santiago Rojas Mejía</Say>

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| *n.º* | number (abbreviation of *número*) |
| *realizar un pedido* | to place an order |
| *días laborables* | working days (*días hábiles* in much of Latin America) |
| *el depósito* | (water) tank |
| *ponerse en contacto con* | to get in touch with |
| *proceder a* | to proceed to, to go ahead with |
| *el importe* | amount, sum |
| *en su defecto* | failing that |
| *el coste* | cost (*el costo* in Latin America) |
| *la candidatura* | application, candidacy |
| *el monitor, la monitora* | instructor, activity leader |
| *el docente* | teacher (formal) |
| *el enfoque* | approach |
| *la divulgación* | popularisation (of science) |
| *la hoja de vida* | CV (Colombia and elsewhere) |
| *de antemano* | in advance |

**Things to notice.** Both writers use *usted/ustedes* forms throughout (*les ruego que procedan, su tienda, su museo, le adjunto*). The complaint is built with formal connectors (*sin embargo, por consiguiente, en su defecto*) and impersonal structures from <LessonLink slug="science-and-the-impersonal">Science and the impersonal</LessonLink> (*debía entregarse, puede observarse, se me aseguró*); it reports a conversation in indirect speech (*se me aseguró que recibiría*). The cover letter swaps everyday verbs for formal ones (*presentar mi candidatura, ampliar información*) and closes with two set phrases.

</Section>

<Section id="pronunciation" kind="pronunciation" title="Reading abbreviations and addresses aloud">

Formal texts are full of abbreviations, and when you read them aloud you say the full word:

| Written | Said |
| --- | --- |
| Sr., Sra., Srta. | <Say lang="es">señor, señora, señorita</Say> |
| Ud., Uds. | <Say lang="es">usted, ustedes</Say> |
| Dr., Dra. | <Say lang="es">doctor, doctora</Say> |
| Lic. | <Say lang="es">licenciado, licenciada</Say> |
| n.º | <Say lang="es">número</Say> |
| p. ej. | <Say lang="es">por ejemplo</Say> |
| etc. | <Say lang="es">etcétera</Say> |
| Avda., Av. | <Say lang="es">avenida</Say> |
| C/ (Spain) | <Say lang="es">calle</Say> |

Email addresses are spelled out with a few special words: `@` is <Say lang="es">arroba</Say>, `.` is <Say lang="es">punto</Say>, `-` is <Say lang="es">guion</Say> and `_` is <Say lang="es">guion bajo</Say>. So `c.llorente@ejemplo.es` is read *ce punto llorente arroba ejemplo punto es*. The letter *w* is <Say lang="es">uve doble</Say> in Spain and <Say lang="es">doble ve</Say> or <Say lang="es">doble u</Say> in much of Latin America.

Numbers also differ: Spain and many South American countries write *1.250,50* (a point for thousands, a comma for decimals), while Mexico and several other countries write *1,250.50*, as in English. Check which convention your reader uses before writing amounts.

</Section>

<Section id="culture" kind="culture" title="Formality across the Spanish-speaking world">

How formal to be depends a great deal on the country and the situation. Emails between colleagues in Spain often begin with a simple *Hola, Marta:* and end with *Un saludo*, while in many Latin American countries even internal emails keep *Estimada Marta:* and *Cordialmente*. When writing to someone for the first time, start formal; it's easy to relax later and awkward to recover from being too casual.

Titles matter in some places. In Mexico, *Lic.* (for a university graduate), *Ing.* (engineer) and *Mtro./Mtra.* (master's degree holder) are commonly used in professional correspondence. In Colombia, some people address each other with *usted* even in the family, and in parts of the country you will hear the old, respectful *sumercé* (from *su merced*).

If your formal letter is a complaint, both Spain and Latin America have institutions behind you. In Spain, shops and businesses must have official complaint forms, the *hojas de reclamaciones*, available to customers. In Mexico, consumer complaints can be taken to PROFECO, the federal consumer protection office. Other countries have similar bodies.

</Section>

<Section id="misconceptions" kind="misconceptions" title="Common mistakes">

<Misconception>

**"Estimada señora López,"** In Spanish the greeting of a letter is followed by a **colon**: *Estimada señora López:* The comma is an English habit, though you will see it in informal emails.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"Querido señor Pérez" for a business letter.** *Querido* (dear) implies affection and is for people you know well. In formal letters use *Estimado*.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"Sinceramente" as a closing.** It's a word-for-word copy of *Sincerely*, and isn't a standard sign-off. Use *Atentamente*, *Cordialmente* or *Un cordial saludo*.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"Aplicar a un puesto."** This is an anglicism, heard in parts of Latin America but avoided in careful writing. Use ***solicitar un puesto***, ***presentar mi candidatura***, or in Latin America ***postularse a un puesto***.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"19 de Noviembre."** Months and days of the week are not capitalised in Spanish: *19 de noviembre*, *el lunes*.

</Misconception>

</Section>
