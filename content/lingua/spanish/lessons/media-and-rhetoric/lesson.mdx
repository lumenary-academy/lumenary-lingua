<Section id="press-grammar" kind="grammar" title="The grammar of the press">

### Headlines

Spanish headlines compress, and they have their own conventions (the examples here are invented):

- **The present for past events**, for immediacy: *El Congreso **aprueba** la reforma de las pensiones* (it did so yesterday).
- **The future for planned events**: *El metro **cerrará** tres estaciones en agosto.*
- **Missing articles and verbs**: *Récord de visitantes en el museo de la ciudad.* · *Detenidos dos sospechosos del robo.*
- **A colon for attribution**: *La ministra de Sanidad: «No habrá recortes».*
- **The comma for an omitted verb**: *El Gobierno, contra la subida de tarifas* (= *está contra*).
- **Only the first word capitalised**: *Nueva ley de vivienda en Uruguay*, never *Nueva Ley De Vivienda*.

### The conditional of rumour

*El exministro **habría cobrado** comisiones* does not mean "would have received" but "**allegedly** received": the paper reports a claim it has not confirmed. This *condicional de rumor* is extremely common, but many style books advise against it because it lets a paper spread a claim without owning it. Careful journalism prefers to name the source: ***Según** la Fiscalía, el exministro cobró comisiones.*

### Who did it? Agents and their disappearance

Grammar lets a writer show or hide responsibility, and Spanish has more tools for hiding it than English:

| Version | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| *La policía disolvió la protesta.* | Agent as subject: responsibility is clear. |
| *La protesta fue disuelta por la policía.* | Passive: the protest is the topic; the agent is still there. |
| *Se disolvió la protesta.* | Passive *se*: no agent at all. |
| *Muere un manifestante durante la protesta.* | Intransitive verb: the death simply "happens". |
| *Disolución de la protesta.* | Nominalisation: no agent, no time. |

None of these is wrong in itself; all can be honest. But when you read *se produjeron incidentes* or *muere un joven tras la intervención*, ask who did what.

### Reporting verbs carry a judgement

| Verb | What it suggests about the speaker's words |
| --- | --- |
| *decir*, *afirmar*, *declarar*, *señalar* | neutral |
| *asegurar*, *sostener* | the speaker insists; doubt hangs in the air |
| *alegar*, *pretender* | the reporter is sceptical |
| *reconocer*, *admitir* | the speaker concedes something unfavourable |
| *denunciar* | the speaker accuses |
| *advertir*, *alertar* | the speaker warns |
| *lamentar* | the speaker regrets |
| *revelar* | the information was hidden |

*La ministra **dijo** que no habrá recortes* and *La ministra **aseguró** que no habrá recortes* report the same words; the second invites you not to believe them.

<Section id="news-opinion" kind="grammar" title="News and opinion">

Spanish-language newspapers separate their genres, at least in principle:

| Genre | What it is | Typical language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *la noticia* | the report of a recent event | third person, past tenses, attributed sources, figures |
| *el reportaje* | an in-depth report | background, several voices, description |
| *la crónica* | an eyewitness account with style | first person allowed, narration, evaluation |
| *la entrevista* | questions and answers | direct speech |
| *el editorial* | the paper's own position, unsigned | *nosotros* or impersonal, modal verbs (*debe*) |
| *la columna* | a regular signed opinion | first person, irony, personal voice |
| *la tribuna* | an occasional signed piece by a guest | argumentative |
| *la carta al director* | a reader's letter | short, direct |

**Markers of opinion**: the first person (*creo*, *a mi juicio*); evaluative adjectives (*vergonzoso*, *valiente*, *inadmisible*); modal verbs (*el Gobierno debería…*); rhetorical questions; irony; exclamations; direct appeals to the reader (*¿Y usted?*); intensifiers of certainty (*sin duda*, *es evidente que*). A news report that contains several of these is no longer quite news.

</Section>

<Section id="persuasion" kind="grammar" title="Persuasion, fallacies and bias">

Persuasion is not manipulation: a good op-ed persuades with evidence and argument. The problem is the technique that *replaces* argument. These are the names you will meet in Spanish; the examples are invented.

| Technique | Spanish name | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Appeal to emotion | *apelación a las emociones* | *¿Quién se atreve a decirle a un niño que su biblioteca cierra?* |
| False dilemma | *falso dilema* | *O mantenemos la biblioteca o renunciamos a la cultura.* |
| Ad hominem | *argumento ad hominem* | *Lo propone un concejal que no ha leído un libro en su vida.* |
| Straw man | *falacia del hombre de paja* | *Los que quieren cerrarla creen que leer no sirve para nada.* |
| Slippery slope | *pendiente resbaladiza* | *Hoy cierran la biblioteca; mañana, el hospital.* |
| Bandwagon | *apelación a la mayoría* | *Todo el mundo sabe que…* |
| Hasty generalisation | *generalización apresurada* | *Mi vecino nunca va: nadie la usa.* |
| Loaded language | *léxico valorativo* | *el tijeretazo* (the axe) vs *el ajuste presupuestario* |
| Euphemism | *eufemismo* | *reestructuración de plantilla* for layoffs |
| Presupposition | *presuposición* | *¿Por qué sigue el alcalde ocultando las cuentas?* (assumes he is hiding them) |
| Cherry-picking | *selección interesada de datos* | quoting the one figure that suits you |

### A checklist for detecting bias

1. **Genre**: is it news or opinion? Is it labelled honestly?
2. **Sources**: who is quoted, who is not? Are sources named (*según la Fiscalía*) or vague (*fuentes cercanas*)?
3. **Verbs**: *dijo* or *alegó*? *reconoció* or *explicó*?
4. **Agents**: who does what — and where has the agent disappeared?
5. **Words**: *protesta* or *disturbios*? *Ajuste* or *recorte*? *Régimen* or *gobierno*?
6. **Numbers**: absolute or relative? Compared with what?
7. **What is missing**: the other side's best argument, context, dates.

</Section>

<Section id="op-ed" kind="reading" title="Reading: one event, two texts">

Both texts were written for this lesson, about a fictional town. First the news report, then a columnist's op-ed on the same decision.

### The news report

> **El Ayuntamiento de Santa Inés cerrará la biblioteca del barrio de La Ribera**
>
> *La medida, incluida en el presupuesto del próximo año, permitirá ahorrar 180 000 euros anuales, según el consistorio*
>
> El pleno municipal de Santa Inés de la Vega aprobó ayer, con 11 votos a favor y 10 en contra, el cierre de la biblioteca del barrio de La Ribera a partir de marzo. Según el concejal de Hacienda, Tomás Ferrer, la medida forma parte de un plan de ajuste que permitirá ahorrar unos 180 000 euros al año. Los fondos se trasladarán a la Biblioteca Central, situada a tres kilómetros. Los grupos de la oposición votaron en contra, y la asociación de vecinos ha convocado una concentración para el próximo sábado. De acuerdo con los datos del propio ayuntamiento, la biblioteca recibió el año pasado unas 40 000 visitas.

Inverted pyramid, past tenses, exact figures, every claim attributed (*según*, *de acuerdo con*), neutral verbs, no adjectives of judgement.

### The op-ed

> **La biblioteca no es un lujo**
>
> *Carmen Oyarzábal*
>
> Hay decisiones que retratan a quien las toma. La del Ayuntamiento de Santa Inés, que ayer aprobó por un solo voto cerrar la biblioteca de La Ribera, es una de ellas.
>
> Empecemos por los números, ya que el concejal de Hacienda tanto los aprecia. El cierre ahorrará 180 000 euros al año. La biblioteca recibió 40 000 visitas el año pasado: cuatro euros y medio por visita. ¿Hay algún servicio público más barato?
>
> El concejal asegura que los vecinos podrán usar la Biblioteca Central. Se le olvida mencionar que está a tres kilómetros, que el autobús pasa cada cuarenta minutos y que buena parte de los usuarios de La Ribera son niños y personas mayores.
>
> Quienes defienden el cierre nos dicen que hoy todo está en internet y que el papel es cosa del pasado. Pero una biblioteca no es un almacén de libros: es el único lugar del barrio donde un estudiante sin conexión puede hacer los deberes, donde una mujer recién llegada aprende a rellenar un formulario, donde nadie te pide que consumas para quedarte.
>
> Que nadie se engañe: hoy es la biblioteca; mañana será el centro de salud, y pasado mañana, la escuela. O defendemos los servicios públicos ahora, o renunciaremos a ellos para siempre.
>
> Por cierto, no deja de ser curioso que el concejal Ferrer, tan preocupado por el gasto, no haya pisado una biblioteca en años, según cuentan en el barrio.
>
> Todo el mundo sabe que una ciudad que cierra bibliotecas es una ciudad que renuncia al futuro. El sábado, los vecinos de La Ribera se concentrarán frente al ayuntamiento. Yo estaré allí. ¿Y usted?

### Taking it apart

| Passage | Technique | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Hay decisiones que retratan a quien las toma.* | Evaluative framing in the first line | Legitimate in opinion, but it judges before arguing. |
| *por un solo voto* | Selection: the news said *11 a 10* | Accurate, but chosen to suggest weakness. |
| *cuatro euros y medio por visita* | Argument from data | Sound and checkable (180 000 ÷ 40 000 = 4.5), though visits are not users. |
| *Se le olvida mencionar que…* | Irony; relevant counter-evidence | Fair: distance and bus frequency matter. |
| *Quienes defienden el cierre nos dicen que…* | Straw man | The council never said this; its argument was cost. |
| *donde…, donde…, donde…* | Anaphora, concrete examples | The strongest argument in the piece. |
| *hoy es la biblioteca; mañana…* | Slippery slope | No evidence offered. |
| *O defendemos… o renunciaremos…* | False dilemma | Ignores intermediate options (reduced hours, shared building). |
| *no haya pisado una biblioteca… según cuentan* | Ad hominem plus an unverified source | Irrelevant to the argument and unconfirmed. |
| *Todo el mundo sabe que…* | Appeal to the majority | Asserts instead of arguing. |
| *Yo estaré allí. ¿Y usted?* | Call to action, rhetorical question | Typical and legitimate close for a column. |

The verdict is mixed, which is the point: the op-ed contains a good argument (cost per visit, access, what a library does) wrapped in techniques that a careful reader should discount. A fair-minded columnist could make the same case without the last four.

</Section>

<Section id="press-landscape" kind="culture" title="The Spanish-language press">

Every Spanish-speaking country has its newspapers of record, and many are over a century old: *El País*, *El Mundo*, *ABC* and *La Vanguardia* in Spain; *Clarín* and *La Nación* in Argentina; *El Universal*, *Reforma* and *La Jornada* in Mexico; *El Tiempo* in Colombia; *El Mercurio* in Chile; *El Comercio* in Peru. They differ in editorial line, and reading the same story in two of them is the best exercise in detecting bias you can do. Much of the international news in Spanish comes from the agency **EFE**, the largest Spanish-language news agency.

The language of the press has its own guardians. Major papers publish **style books** (*libros de estilo*) that rule on everything from headline capitals to the conditional of rumour, and the **Fundéu RAE**, set up by EFE and now run with the Real Academia Española, answers journalists' doubts every day. Since the 2010s, **fact-checking** organisations have grown across the Spanish-speaking world — among them Chequeado in Argentina, Colombiacheck in Colombia, and Maldita.es and Newtral in Spain — and their published methods are a good model of how to verify a claim before sharing it.

</Section>

<Section id="misconceptions" kind="misconceptions" title="Common misconceptions">

<Misconception>

**"News with no adjectives is objective."** Bias also lives in the choice of story, the order of facts, the verbs of reporting, the sources quoted and the agent left out. The news report above is careful; even so, putting *11 votos a favor y 10 en contra* rather than *por un solo voto* is itself a choice.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"*Habría cobrado* means 'would have received'."** In the press, the conditional often means "reportedly / allegedly". Read it as a warning: the claim is unconfirmed.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"*La editorial* is an editorial."** *La editorial* publishes books; *el editorial* is the newspaper's opinion piece.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"A quoted source is a confirmed fact."** *Según fuentes cercanas al ministerio* tells you someone said it, not that it is true. Ask how many sources, and whether any is named.

</Misconception>

</Section>
