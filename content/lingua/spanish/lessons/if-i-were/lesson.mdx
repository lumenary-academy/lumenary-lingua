<Section id="formation" kind="grammar" title="Forming the imperfect subjunctive">

Every verb, regular or not, builds its imperfect subjunctive the same way:

1. Take the **ellos** form of the **preterite**: *hablaron, comieron, tuvieron, fueron*.
2. Remove **-ron**: *habla-, comie-, tuvie-, fue-*.
3. Add **-ra** endings (or **-se** endings).

| | hablar → hablaron | comer → comieron | vivir → vivieron |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| yo | hablara / hablase | comiera / comiese | viviera / viviese |
| tú | hablaras / hablases | comieras / comieses | vivieras / vivieses |
| él, ella, usted | hablara / hablase | comiera / comiese | viviera / viviese |
| nosotros | habláramos / hablásemos | comiéramos / comiésemos | viviéramos / viviésemos |
| vosotros | hablarais / hablaseis | comierais / comieseis | vivierais / vivieseis |
| ellos, ustedes | hablaran / hablasen | comieran / comiesen | vivieran / viviesen |

Because it starts from the preterite, every irregularity you learned in <LessonLink slug="trips-and-irregular-pasts">Trips and irregular pasts</LessonLink> comes along:

| Infinitive | Preterite (ellos) | Imperfect subjunctive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| tener | tuvieron | tuviera |
| estar | estuvieron | estuviera |
| poder | pudieron | pudiera |
| saber | supieron | supiera |
| hacer | hicieron | hiciera |
| querer | quisieron | quisiera |
| venir | vinieron | viniera |
| decir | dijeron | dijera |
| traer | trajeron | trajera |
| ser, ir | fueron | fuera |
| dar | dieron | diera |
| haber | hubieron | hubiera |
| pedir | pidieron | pidiera |
| dormir | durmieron | durmiera |
| leer | leyeron | leyera |

Note *dijera, trajera, condujera*: no *i* after the *j*, because there is none in *dijeron*. And *fuera* belongs to both *ser* and *ir*; context decides.

### -ra or -se?

The two sets of forms are interchangeable in almost every use in this lesson: *si tuviera* = *si tuviese*. The **-ra** forms are more frequent everywhere; the **-se** forms are used more in Spain than in Latin America, and more in writing than in speech. The one exception: when the *-ra* form replaces a conditional to sound polite (*quisiera, debiera, pudiera*), only *-ra* is used.

</Section>

<Section id="conditionals" kind="grammar" title="If I had…, I would…">

### Real and unreal conditions

Spanish, like English, distinguishes conditions that are real or likely from conditions that are imagined:

| Type | *si* clause | Main clause | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Real or likely | present indicative | present, future, *ir a*, command | <Say lang="es">Si mañana hace sol, iremos a la playa.</Say> |
| Unlikely or imagined (present, future) | imperfect subjunctive | conditional | <Say lang="es">Si hiciera sol, iríamos a la playa.</Say> |

*Si hace sol* means the sun is a real possibility; *si hiciera sol* means it isn't sunny now, or probably won't be. The choice is the speaker's: the same situation can be presented either way.

- <Say lang="es">Si tuviera más tiempo, aprendería a tocar el charango.</Say> — If I had more time, I'd learn to play the charango.
- <Say lang="es">Si fuera tú, aceptaría la oferta.</Say> — If I were you, I'd accept the offer. (= *Yo que tú, aceptaría…*)
- <Say lang="es">¿Qué harías si te tocara la lotería?</Say> — What would you do if you won the lottery?
- <Say lang="es">No lo haría ni aunque me pagaran.</Say> — I wouldn't do it even if they paid me.

The order of the two clauses is free; when the *si* clause comes first, it is followed by a comma.

<Note>

After *si* meaning "if", Spanish never uses the present subjunctive (*si tenga*) or, in standard usage, the conditional (*si tendría*). You will hear *si tendría* in speech in some areas, such as the Basque Country and neighbouring regions of Spain, but it is avoided in careful Spanish. The rule of thumb: the "would" belongs in the main clause, never after *si*.

</Note>

A more formal alternative replaces *si* + imperfect subjunctive with ***de* + infinitive**: <Say lang="es">De tener dinero, viajaría más.</Say> (If I had money, I'd travel more.)

### Como si: as if

*Como si* is always followed by the subjunctive, and in the present it takes the **imperfect** subjunctive, even though English sometimes uses a present:

- <Say lang="es">Habla como si lo supiera todo.</Say> — He talks as if he knew everything.
- <Say lang="es">Me miró como si no me conociera.</Say> — She looked at me as if she didn't know me.
- <Say lang="es">Gasta dinero como si fuera millonario.</Say> — He spends money as if he were a millionaire.

### Ojalá with the imperfect subjunctive

In <LessonLink slug="feelings-and-doubt">Feelings and doubt</LessonLink> you used *ojalá* with the present subjunctive for wishes that can come true. With the **imperfect** subjunctive, the wish becomes unlikely or impossible:

| Possible (present subjunctive) | Unlikely or impossible (imperfect subjunctive) |
| --- | --- |
| *Ojalá tenga tiempo mañana.* I hope I'll have time tomorrow. | *Ojalá tuviera tiempo.* I wish I had time (but I don't). |
| *Ojalá venga Ana.* I hope Ana comes. | *Ojalá viniera Ana.* If only Ana would come (she probably won't). |

### Quisiera: the most polite request

*Quisiera* (from *querer*) is even softer than *me gustaría*: <Say lang="es">Quisiera hacerle una pregunta.</Say> (I'd like to ask you a question.)

</Section>

<Section id="sequence-of-tenses" kind="grammar" title="The sequence of tenses">

In <LessonLink slug="wishes-and-advice">Wishes and advice</LessonLink> the main verb was in the present, so the subjunctive was present too. When the main verb moves into the **past** or the **conditional**, the subjunctive normally moves into the **imperfect**. Spanish grammar calls this the *correlación de tiempos*.

| Main verb | Subjunctive | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present, future, present perfect, command | present | <Say lang="es">Quiero que vengas.</Say> *Te pediré que me ayudes.* |
| Preterite, imperfect, pluperfect | imperfect | <Say lang="es">Quería que vinieras.</Say> *Le pedí que me ayudara.* |
| Conditional | imperfect | <Say lang="es">Me gustaría que vinieras.</Say> *Sería mejor que te quedaras.* |

- <Say lang="es">Mis padres esperaban que estudiara Derecho.</Say> — My parents hoped I'd study law.
- <Say lang="es">Era importante que llegáramos a tiempo.</Say> — It was important for us to arrive on time.
- <Say lang="es">No creía que fuera tan difícil.</Say> — I didn't think it would be so hard.
- <Say lang="es">Me alegró mucho que me llamaras.</Say> — I was really glad you called me.

<Note>

When what was asked or wished in the past still lies in the future, a present subjunctive is also possible after a past verb, and is common in Latin American speech: *Me pidió que lo llame mañana.* The imperfect, *que lo llamara*, is always correct.

</Note>

</Section>

<Section id="reading" kind="reading" title="Reading: Si tuviera un año libre">

**A short personal essay, written for this lesson.** Marisol, a nurse from Cusco in Peru, writes about a question a colleague asked her.

> <Say lang="es">Me llamo Marisol, tengo cuarenta y dos años y soy enfermera en un hospital de Cusco. Hace unos días, en la pausa del café, una compañera nos preguntó qué haríamos si tuviéramos un año entero libre, con el sueldo pagado. Todas se rieron, pero yo no he dejado de pensar en la pregunta.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Si tuviera un año libre, lo primero que haría sería dormir. No una noche, sino un mes entero, como si el despertador no existiera. Después aprendería por fin a tocar el charango. Mi abuelo lo tocaba en todas las fiestas y siempre me decía que yo tenía buen oído. Ojalá estuviera vivo para enseñarme; si pudiera elegir un maestro, sería él.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">También viajaría, pero no muy lejos. Me gustaría recorrer los pueblos del valle donde nació mi madre, sentarme con la gente mayor y escribir sus historias en quechua y en castellano. Mi madre dice que, cuando ella era niña, los mayores contaban cuentos que duraban hasta la madrugada, y que hoy casi nadie los recuerda. Hay tantas cosas que nadie ha apuntado nunca…</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Mi marido dice que me aburriría en dos semanas. Puede que tenga razón: si no tuviera pacientes a los que cuidar, no sabría muy bien quién soy. Por eso, cuando lo pienso con calma, me doy cuenta de que lo que de verdad querría no es un año sin trabajo, sino una vida en la que no hiciera falta escaparse.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Si mi jefa leyera esto, me diría que volviera al trabajo. Y tendría razón: son las siete y empieza mi turno.</Say>

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| *la pausa del café* | coffee break |
| *el sueldo pagado* | on full pay |
| *no he dejado de pensar en* | I haven't stopped thinking about |
| *el despertador* | alarm clock |
| *el charango* | a small Andean string instrument |
| *tener buen oído* | to have a good ear (for music) |
| *recorrer* | to travel around, to go through |
| *la gente mayor* | older people |
| *el quechua* | Quechua, an Indigenous language of the Andes, widely spoken in Peru |
| *el castellano* | Spanish (the usual name for it in Peru and many other places) |
| *la madrugada* | the small hours, before dawn |
| *apuntar* | to write down, to note |
| *los pacientes a los que cuidar* | patients to look after |
| *escaparse* | to escape, to get away |
| *el turno* | shift |

**Things to notice.** Every imagined situation pairs *si* + imperfect subjunctive with the conditional (*si tuviera…, haría; si pudiera…, sería; si no tuviera…, no sabría; si mi jefa leyera…, me diría*). *Como si el despertador no existiera* and *ojalá estuviera vivo* use the imperfect subjunctive for the impossible. And the sequence of tenses is everywhere: *preguntó qué haríamos si tuviéramos*, *querría… una vida en la que no hiciera falta*, *me diría que volviera*.

</Section>

<Section id="pronunciation" kind="pronunciation" title="Stress in the -ra forms">

The imperfect subjunctive is stressed on the syllable **before** *-ra* or *-se*: *ha-BLA-ra, tu-VIE-ra, pu-DIE-se*. In the *nosotros* form that syllable ends up third from last, and Spanish writes an accent on every word stressed there (an *esdrújula*). So the *nosotros* forms always carry an accent:

<Say lang="es">habláramos</Say>, <Say lang="es">tuviéramos</Say>, <Say lang="es">fuéramos</Say>, <Say lang="es">pudiésemos</Say>.

Don't confuse the *-ra* forms with the future, which looks similar on paper but is stressed on the ending: <Say lang="es">hablara</Say> (*ha-BLA-ra*, that I spoke) and <Say lang="es">hablará</Say> (*ha-bla-RÁ*, she will speak). The written accent, again, tells you which is which.

One more homograph: *fuera* is both the imperfect subjunctive of *ser* and *ir* and the adverb "outside" (*Los niños juegan fuera*). They sound identical: <Say lang="es">si fuera fácil</Say>, <Say lang="es">cenamos fuera</Say>.

</Section>

<Section id="culture" kind="culture" title="Y si me tocara el Gordo…">

Every year on 22 December, Spain stops to listen to the **Sorteo Extraordinario de Navidad**, the Christmas lottery draw. Children from Madrid's Colegio de San Ildefonso sing the winning numbers and prizes for hours, and the top prize is known as **el Gordo** ("the fat one"). Tickets are often shared among families, colleagues and regulars of the same bar, so a winning number can make a whole office or village celebrate at once. In the weeks before, the question on everyone's lips is a second-type conditional: *¿Qué harías si te tocara el Gordo?*

Many other countries have their national lotteries, such as Mexico's Lotería Nacional; Mexico also has a much-loved board game called *lotería*, played with picture cards.

Everyday Spanish also uses the imperfect subjunctive in a few expressive phrases worth recognising:

- *¡Ni que fuera…!* — expresses disbelief: *¿Por qué grita tanto? ¡Ni que fuera sordo!* (Why is he shouting so much? It's not as if he were deaf!)
- *Como si nada* — as if nothing had happened: *Llegó tarde y se sentó como si nada.*
- *Como si fuera poco* — as if that weren't enough: *Perdí el tren y, como si fuera poco, empezó a llover.*

</Section>

<Section id="misconceptions" kind="misconceptions" title="Common mistakes">

<Misconception>

**"Si tendría tiempo, iría."** The conditional goes in the main clause, not after *si*: ***Si tuviera** tiempo, iría.*

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"Si tenga tiempo, iré."** After *si* meaning "if", there is no present subjunctive. For a real possibility, use the present indicative: ***Si tengo** tiempo, iré.*

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"Habla como si sabe todo."** *Como si* always takes the subjunctive, and for the present it is the imperfect: *Habla como si **supiera** todo.*

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"Dijiera, trajiera."** The imperfect subjunctive copies the preterite stem, and *dijeron, trajeron* have no *i*: ***dijera, trajera***.

</Misconception>

</Section>
