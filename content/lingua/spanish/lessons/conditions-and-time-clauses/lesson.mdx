<Section id="vocabulary" kind="vocabulary" title="Words that link clauses">

This lesson is about the small words that join two clauses: *cuando, para que, aunque, a menos que*. After some of them Spanish always uses the subjunctive; after others it depends on meaning. It also completes the picture of conditional sentences from <LessonLink slug="if-i-were">If I were…</LessonLink> by moving them into the past: *if I had known, I would have…*

<Section id="time-clauses" kind="grammar" title="Time clauses: pending or not?">

After *cuando* and the other time conjunctions, ask one question: **has the event happened (or does it happen regularly), or is it still pending?**

- Pending, in the future from the speaker's point of view → **subjunctive**.
- A habit, a general truth, or something in the past → **indicative**.

| Pending → subjunctive | Habit or past → indicative |
| --- | --- |
| <Say lang="es">Cuando llegues, llámame.</Say> | <Say lang="es">Cuando llego a casa, siempre llamo a mi madre.</Say> |
| *En cuanto termine, te ayudo.* | *En cuanto terminé, me fui.* |
| *Esperaré hasta que vuelvas.* | *Esperé hasta que volviste.* |
| *Mientras vivas en mi casa, ayudarás.* (as long as) | *Mientras vivía en Lima, aprendí a cocinar.* |

English uses a present (*when you arrive*) where Spanish uses a subjunctive. And Spanish never uses the future after *cuando*: *cuando llegarás* is a typical learner's mistake.

The same logic works in the past. If, from a past point of view, the event was still to come, use the **imperfect** subjunctive: <Say lang="es">Me dijo que me llamaría cuando llegara.</Say> (She said she'd call me when she arrived.)

**Antes de que** always takes the subjunctive, because the event hasn't happened yet at the moment of the main verb: <Say lang="es">Sal antes de que empiece a llover.</Say> <Say lang="es">Salimos antes de que empezara a llover.</Say> (The proverb *Antes que te cases, mira lo que haces* shows that *antes que* without *de* is also correct.)

If both clauses have the **same subject**, use a preposition and an infinitive instead: *antes de salir, después de comer, hasta terminar*. <Say lang="es">Antes de salir, cierra la ventana.</Say>

</Section>

<Section id="purpose-and-condition" kind="grammar" title="Purpose, condition and concession">

### Always subjunctive

These conjunctions always introduce something not yet real, so they always take the subjunctive:

- **para que** (so that): <Say lang="es">Te lo explico para que lo entiendas.</Say>
- **sin que** (without): <Say lang="es">Salió sin que nadie la viera.</Say>
- **a menos que, a no ser que** (unless): <Say lang="es">Iremos en bici, a menos que llueva.</Say>
- **con tal de que, siempre que, siempre y cuando** (provided that): <Say lang="es">Te presto el coche con tal de que lo devuelvas lleno.</Say>
- **en caso de que** (in case, if): *En caso de que haya algún problema, avísame.*

As with *antes de*, a shared subject means an infinitive: *Ahorro **para viajar*** (I save so I can travel) but *Ahorro **para que mis hijos viajen*** (so that my children can travel). *Se fue **sin decir** nada* but *Se fue **sin que** nadie **dijera** nada.*

### Aunque: two meanings

**Aunque** + indicative presents a fact: *although*. **Aunque** + subjunctive presents a hypothesis, or a fact the speaker treats as irrelevant: *even if, even though*.

| Aunque + indicative | Aunque + subjunctive |
| --- | --- |
| <Say lang="es">Aunque llueve, voy a salir.</Say> It is raining, but I'm going out. | <Say lang="es">Aunque llueva, voy a salir.</Say> Even if it rains, I'm going out. |
| *Aunque es caro, lo voy a comprar.* (I know it's expensive.) | *Aunque sea caro, lo voy a comprar.* (Whatever it costs.) |
| | *Aunque lloviera, saldría.* Even if it were to rain, I'd go out. |

### Por si: indicative

*Por si* (in case) is the exception that catches many learners. It takes the **indicative** in the present: <Say lang="es">Llévate un paraguas por si llueve.</Say> (*Por si lloviera*, with the imperfect subjunctive, is possible for something less likely.)

</Section>

<Section id="compound-tenses" kind="grammar" title="What will have happened, and what would have happened">

You already know *haber* + participle in the present perfect and the pluperfect. Three more compound tenses complete the set.

| Tense | haber in… | Example | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Future perfect | future: *habré, habrás, habrá, habremos, habréis, habrán* | <Say lang="es">Para junio habré terminado la carrera.</Say> | Done before a future point; a guess about the past |
| Conditional perfect | conditional: *habría, habrías, habría, habríamos, habríais, habrían* | <Say lang="es">Yo habría dicho que sí.</Say> | What would have happened |
| Pluperfect subjunctive | imperfect subjunctive: *hubiera / hubiese, hubieras…* | <Say lang="es">Si lo hubiera sabido…</Say> | Unreal past, after *si, ojalá, como si* |

The future perfect also makes a **guess about the past**, just as the simple future guesses about the present: <Say lang="es">No contesta. Habrá salido.</Say> (She's not answering. She must have gone out.)

### Third-type conditionals

To imagine a different past, use *si* + **pluperfect subjunctive**, and the **conditional perfect** in the main clause:

- <Say lang="es">Si hubiera sabido que venías, habría hecho una tarta.</Say> — If I'd known you were coming, I'd have made a cake.
- <Say lang="es">Si no hubiéramos perdido el tren, habríamos llegado a tiempo.</Say> — If we hadn't missed the train, we'd have arrived on time.

In the main clause, the pluperfect subjunctive can replace the conditional perfect, and is very common in speech: *Si hubiera sabido que venías, **hubiera hecho** una tarta.*

| Condition | *si* clause | Main clause | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Real | present indicative | present, future, command | *Si estudias, aprobarás.* |
| Unreal present or unlikely future | imperfect subjunctive | conditional | *Si estudiaras, aprobarías.* |
| Unreal past | pluperfect subjunctive | conditional perfect (or pluperfect subjunctive) | *Si hubieras estudiado, habrías aprobado.* |

### Mixed conditionals

The two halves can belong to different times:

- Past condition, present result: <Say lang="es">Si hubiera estudiado Medicina, ahora sería médica.</Say>
- Present condition, past result: <Say lang="es">Si fuera más organizado, no habría perdido el pasaporte.</Say>

### Regrets and unreal comparisons

*Ojalá* and *como si* also take the pluperfect subjunctive to talk about the past:

- <Say lang="es">Ojalá hubiera ido al concierto.</Say> — I wish I'd gone to the concert.
- <Say lang="es">Me habló como si no hubiera pasado nada.</Say> — He spoke to me as if nothing had happened.

And in formal or written Spanish, ***de* + perfect infinitive** replaces the *si* clause: <Say lang="es">De haberlo sabido, habría venido antes.</Say> (Had I known, I'd have come earlier.)

</Section>

<Section id="reading" kind="reading" title="Reading: Lo que me habría gustado saber">

**A blog post, written for this lesson.** Felipe, from Medellín in Colombia, has lived in Valencia, Spain, for three years.

> <Say lang="es">Cuando llegué a Valencia hace tres años, creía que lo tenía todo controlado. Había leído decenas de foros, había hecho listas y había ahorrado durante meses. Aun así, si alguien me hubiera dado estos consejos antes de que subiera al avión, me habría ahorrado más de un disgusto. Aquí van, por si te sirven.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Primero: no vendas todo antes de que te confirmen el trabajo. Yo vendí hasta la bicicleta, y en cuanto encontré piso tuve que comprar otra.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Segundo: trae copias de todos tus documentos, aunque te parezca exagerado. Si hubiera traído mi título universitario apostillado, no habría tenido que pedirlo desde aquí, lo que me costó dos meses de correos y llamadas.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Tercero: no esperes a entenderlo todo para salir de casa. Yo hablaba español, claro, pero las primeras semanas no entendía la mitad de lo que decían mis vecinos, que entre ellos hablaban valenciano. Si me hubiera quedado en casa hasta que lo entendiera todo, todavía estaría encerrado.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Cuarto: cuando llegues, apúntate a algo, sea un coro, un equipo o un curso. Yo me apunté a un grupo de senderismo en cuanto vi un cartel en la biblioteca, y allí conocí a casi todos mis amigos de hoy.</Say>
>
> <Say lang="es">Y por último, ten paciencia contigo mismo. Para cuando leas esto, seguramente ya habrás hecho más de lo que crees. A mí me costó un año sentirme en casa. Si me hubieran dicho que iba a tardar tanto, quizá no habría venido… y me habría perdido la mejor decisión de mi vida.</Say>

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| *tenerlo todo controlado* | to have everything under control |
| *decenas de* | dozens of |
| *aun así* | even so |
| *el disgusto* | upset, unpleasant surprise |
| *por si te sirven* | in case they're useful to you |
| *el título universitario* | university degree certificate |
| *apostillado* | certified with an apostille, for official use abroad |
| *el valenciano* | Valencian, the regional language of Valencia |
| *encerrado* | shut in |
| *apuntarse a* | to sign up for, to join |
| *el senderismo* | hiking |
| *el cartel* | poster |
| *para cuando leas esto* | by the time you read this |
| *perderse algo* | to miss out on something |

**Things to notice.** Pending events take the subjunctive (*antes de que te confirmen, cuando llegues, para cuando leas*), past facts the indicative (*en cuanto encontré, en cuanto vi*). *Aunque te parezca exagerado* treats the objection as irrelevant. The third-type conditionals (*si alguien me hubiera dado…, me habría ahorrado; si me hubieran dicho…, no habría venido*) sit next to a mixed one with a present result (*si me hubiera quedado…, todavía estaría encerrado*), and *ya habrás hecho* is the future perfect.

</Section>

<Section id="pronunciation" kind="pronunciation" title="The melody of a conditional sentence">

Long sentences with a condition have a typical melody in Spanish. The voice **rises** at the end of the first part, the *si* clause, to show that the sentence isn't finished; then it **falls** at the end of the main clause. Try it:

- <Say lang="es">Si hubiera sabido que venías, habría hecho una tarta.</Say> (rise on *venías*, fall on *tarta*)
- <Say lang="es">Aunque llueva, voy a salir.</Say> (rise on *llueva*, fall on *salir*)
- <Say lang="es">Cuando llegues, llámame.</Say> (rise on *llegues*, fall on *llámame*)

The forms of *haber* are full of *b* between vowels: *hubiera, habría, habrás*. Between vowels, Spanish *b* and *v* are pronounced softly, with the lips nearly touching but not closing: <Say lang="es">hubiera</Say>, <Say lang="es">habría</Say>. Only after a pause or after *m* and *n* do they become a full English-style *b*: <Say lang="es">un vaso</Say> sounds like *umbaso*.

</Section>

<Section id="culture" kind="culture" title="Refranes: wisdom in conditions and time">

Spanish has a rich store of traditional proverbs, **refranes**, and many use exactly the structures of this lesson. They are anonymous and centuries old, and speakers often quote just the first half, expecting you to know the rest.

- *Antes que te cases, mira lo que haces.* — Before you marry, look at what you're doing. (A pending event: subjunctive.)
- *No dejes para mañana lo que puedas hacer hoy.* — Don't leave for tomorrow what you can do today. (A subjunctive in a relative clause about anything you might do.)
- *Cuando el río suena, agua lleva.* — When the river makes a sound, it's carrying water: where there's smoke, there's fire. (A general truth: indicative after *cuando*.)
- *Más vale tarde que nunca.* — Better late than never.
- *A buenas horas, mangas verdes.* — Used in Spain when help or advice arrives too late; the perfect reply to a *si me lo hubieras dicho antes…*

The Centro Virtual Cervantes keeps a large collection of *refranes* with their meanings and equivalents in other languages; it's worth browsing.

</Section>

<Section id="misconceptions" kind="misconceptions" title="Common mistakes">

<Misconception>

**"Cuando llegarás, llámame."** Spanish never uses the future after *cuando* for a pending event: ***Cuando llegues**, llámame.*

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"Si habría sabido, habría venido."** The *si* clause takes the pluperfect subjunctive: ***Si hubiera sabido**, habría venido.*

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"Aunque always takes the subjunctive."** It depends on meaning. *Aunque está cansada, viene* (she is tired: fact); *aunque esté cansada, vendrá* (even if she's tired).

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"Llévate el paraguas por si llueva."** *Por si* takes the indicative in the present: *por si **llueve***.

</Misconception>

</Section>
