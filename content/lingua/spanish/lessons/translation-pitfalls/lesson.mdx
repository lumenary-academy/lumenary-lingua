<Section id="false-friends" kind="vocabulary" title="False friends">

English and Spanish share thousands of words from Latin, which is a gift until it isn't. A *falso amigo* looks like an English word and means something else. Some are famous jokes; others slip unnoticed into the writing of very advanced speakers, and into machine translation.

| Spanish | Means | Not | For the English word, say |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <Say lang="es">embarazada</Say> | pregnant | embarrassed | *avergonzado*; *me da vergüenza* |
| <Say lang="es">actual</Say> | current | actual | *real*, *verdadero* |
| <Say lang="es">actualmente</Say> | currently | actually | *en realidad*, *de hecho* |
| <Say lang="es">realizar</Say> | to carry out, fulfil | to realise | *darse cuenta de* |
| <Say lang="es">el éxito</Say> | success | exit | *la salida* |
| <Say lang="es">el suceso</Say> | event, incident | success | *el éxito* |
| <Say lang="es">la librería</Say> | bookshop | library | *la biblioteca* |
| <Say lang="es">constipado</Say> | having a cold (Spain) | constipated | *estreñido* |
| <Say lang="es">sensible</Say> | sensitive | sensible | *sensato*, *razonable* |
| <Say lang="es">asistir a</Say> | to attend | to assist (in everyday use) | *ayudar* |
| <Say lang="es">atender</Say> | to serve, attend to, pay attention | to attend | *asistir a* |
| <Say lang="es">eventual</Say> | possible; temporary | eventual | *final* |
| <Say lang="es">eventualmente</Say> | possibly; occasionally | eventually | *finalmente*, *con el tiempo* |
| <Say lang="es">excitado</Say> | aroused; agitated | excited | *emocionado*, *entusiasmado*, *ilusionado* |

| <Say lang="es">pretender</Say> | to aim, try to | to pretend | *fingir* |
| <Say lang="es">resumir</Say> | to summarise | to resume | *reanudar* |
| <Say lang="es">introducir</Say> | to insert, bring in | to introduce (a person) | *presentar* |
| <Say lang="es">la carpeta</Say> | folder | carpet | *la alfombra*, *la moqueta* |

| <Say lang="es">la decepción</Say> | disappointment | deception | *el engaño* |
| <Say lang="es">contestar</Say> | to answer | to contest | *impugnar*, *disputar* |
| <Say lang="es">remover</Say> | to stir | to remove | *quitar*, *eliminar* |

A few are **partial** false friends: *discutir* means "to argue" as well as "to discuss"; *el soporte* is a physical support or a medium (*soporte digital*), and only in IT jargon "technical support" (*soporte técnico*) — for moral support say *apoyo*.

<Section id="calques" kind="grammar" title="Calques and anglicisms">

A **calque** (*calco*) copies a foreign structure with native words. The words are Spanish; the phrase is not. Many come from English, especially in the United States, in business and in technology.

| Calque | Standard Spanish | English source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *aplicar para un trabajo* | *solicitar un puesto*, *postularse a un puesto* | to apply for a job |
| *hacer sentido* | *tener sentido* | to make sense |
| *tomar lugar* | *tener lugar*, *celebrarse* | to take place |
| *jugar un rol* | *desempeñar un papel* | to play a role |
| *en adición a* | *además de* | in addition to |
| *estar supuesto a* | *se supone que* | to be supposed to |
| *llamar para atrás* | *devolver la llamada* | to call back |
| *hacer una decisión* | *tomar una decisión* | to make a decision |
| *es por esto que* | *por eso*, *por esta razón* | this is why (widespread; see <LessonLink slug="style-and-register">Style and register</LessonLink>) |
| *en orden a* (= to) | *para*, *a fin de* | in order to |

*Aplicar a un puesto* is widely heard in parts of Latin America and the US, and some speakers accept it; in careful writing, *solicitar* or *postularse* is the safe choice everywhere.

### Structural calques

The deepest anglicisms are not words but habits:

- **Possessives with body parts and clothes.** *Me lavé mis manos* → ***Me lavé las manos**.* Spanish uses the article when the pronoun already shows whose hands they are.
- **Overused subject pronouns.** *Yo creo que yo voy a ir* → ***Creo que voy a ir**.*
- **The *ser* passive everywhere.** *El contrato fue firmado ayer por las partes* is correct but heavy; *Las partes firmaron ayer el contrato* or *Se firmó ayer el contrato* is more natural.
- **Capitals.** Days, months, languages and nationalities are lower case in Spanish: *el lunes 3 de marzo*, *habla inglés*, *una escritora mexicana*. Titles of books and films capitalise only the first word and proper names: *Cien años de soledad*.
- **Numbers.** *5,000* in English is *5000* (no separator in four-digit numbers) or *5 000* in larger figures; Spain traditionally writes decimals with a comma (*3,5*), Mexico and others with a point.

</Section>

<Section id="prepositions" kind="grammar" title="Prepositions">

Prepositions rarely translate one-for-one. Learn them with the verb, not alone.

### Verbs with a different preposition (or none)

| English | Spanish | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| depend **on** | depender **de** | *Depende **de** ti.* |
| consist **of** | consistir **en** | *El examen consiste **en** tres partes.* |
| dream **about / of** | soñar **con** | *Anoche soñé **contigo**.* |
| fall in love **with** | enamorarse **de** | *Se enamoró **de** su vecino.* |
| marry | casarse **con** | *Se casó **con** una abogada.* |
| think **about** | pensar **en** | *Pienso **en** ti.* |
| think **of** (opinion) | pensar **de**, parecer | *¿Qué piensas **de** él? · ¿Qué te parece?* |
| insist **on** | insistir **en** | *Insiste **en** pagar.* |
| look **for** | buscar | *Busco las llaves.* |
| wait **for** | esperar | *Te espero.* |
| ask **for** | pedir | *Pidió la cuenta.* |
| pay **for** | pagar | *Pagué la cena.* |
| listen **to** | escuchar | *Escucha la radio.* |

### Time and place

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| **on** Monday | *el lunes* (no preposition) |
| **in** the morning | *por la mañana*; also *en la mañana* (much of Latin America) and *a la mañana* (River Plate) |
| **at** five o'clock | *a las cinco* |
| **at** home | *en casa* |
| **by** bus, **by** car | *en autobús*, *en coche* / *en carro* (*a pie*, on foot) |
| **on** the radio | *en la radio* or *por la radio* |
| **in** English | *en inglés* |
| full **of** | *lleno de* |

</Section>

<Section id="gerund" kind="grammar" title="The gerund is not -ing">

English *-ing* does the work of a noun, an adjective and a verb. The Spanish gerund (*-ando*, *-iendo*) is essentially adverbial: it tells *how*, *when*, *why* or *under what condition* the main action happens. Three English habits produce non-standard Spanish.

**1. The gerund as a noun.** English subjects and objects in *-ing* become infinitives in Spanish:

- *Smoking is forbidden* → ***Fumar** está prohibido* (not *fumando*).
- *I love swimming* → *Me encanta **nadar***.
- *before leaving, without saying, after eating* → *antes de **salir**, sin **decir**, después de **comer***.

**2. The gerund as an adjective.** *A box containing books* → *una caja **que contiene** libros* (not *una caja conteniendo libros*). Use a relative clause or *con*: *una caja **con** libros*. Exceptions: *agua **hirviendo***, *una casa **ardiendo*** — fixed uses.

**3. The gerund of a later action.** *El ladrón huyó, **siendo detenido** horas después* is a classic error in journalism: the gerund should express an action simultaneous with or before the main verb, not after it. Write *El ladrón huyó **y fue detenido** horas después.*

And one of tense: Spanish does not use the progressive for future plans. *I'm leaving tomorrow* → ***Me voy** mañana* (not *Estoy yéndome mañana*).

**Correct uses** for comparison: manner (*Llegó **corriendo***), simultaneity (*Lo pensé **volviendo** a casa*), cause (***Siendo** tarde, nos fuimos*), condition (***Estudiando** así, aprobarás*), and the periphrases (*estar*, *seguir*, *llevar* + gerund).

</Section>

<Section id="idioms" kind="grammar" title="Translating idioms">

An idiom is translated by its **function**, not its words. Look for the Spanish expression a native speaker would use in the same situation — and if there is none, translate the meaning plainly.

### English → Spanish

| English | Spanish |
| --- | --- |
| It's raining cats and dogs. | *Llueve a cántaros.* · *Está diluviando.* |
| It's a piece of cake. | *Es pan comido.* |
| to kill two birds with one stone | *matar dos pájaros de un tiro* |
| to cost an arm and a leg | *costar un ojo de la cara* |
| when pigs fly | *cuando las ranas críen pelo* |
| to beat around the bush | *andarse con rodeos*; *irse por las ramas* (to digress) |
| to pull someone's leg | *tomarle el pelo a alguien* |
| in the blink of an eye | *en un abrir y cerrar de ojos* |
| Break a leg! | *¡Mucha suerte!* (theatre people say *¡Mucha mierda!*) |

### Spanish → English

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| *estar en las nubes* | to have one's head in the clouds |
| *ponerse las pilas* | to get one's act together (literally "put one's batteries in") |
| *no tener pelos en la lengua* | not to mince one's words |
| *dar gato por liebre* | to con someone (literally "give cat for hare") |
| *ser uña y carne* | to be inseparable |
| *estar hecho polvo* | to be worn out |
| *consultarlo con la almohada* | to sleep on it |
| *a buenas horas, mangas verdes* | (Spain) too little, too late |

</Section>

<Section id="trap-translations" kind="reading" title="Reading: translations with traps">

Two short texts written for this lesson, each translated badly and then well.

### English → Spanish

> **Source:** Dear team, I'm really excited to announce that we've finally realized our goal: the new library app is a success. Actually, we had over 5,000 downloads in the first week. Eventually we want to apply for a regional grant, so please be sensible about what you post. I'll attend the meeting on Monday to discuss the next steps. Thanks for your support!

> **Bad translation:** Querido equipo: Estoy realmente **excitado** de anunciar que finalmente hemos **realizado** nuestra meta: la nueva aplicación de la biblioteca es un **suceso**. **Actualmente**, tuvimos más de **5,000** descargas en la primera semana. **Eventualmente** queremos **aplicar para** una subvención regional, así que por favor sean **sensibles** con lo que publican. **Atenderé** la reunión **en el lunes** para discutir los próximos pasos. ¡Gracias por su **soporte**!

> **Good translation:** Querido equipo: Me alegra muchísimo anunciar que por fin hemos alcanzado nuestro objetivo: la nueva aplicación de la biblioteca es un éxito. De hecho, tuvimos más de 5000 descargas en la primera semana. Más adelante queremos solicitar una subvención regional, así que, por favor, sean prudentes con lo que publican. Asistiré a la reunión del lunes para tratar los próximos pasos. ¡Gracias por su apoyo!

Eleven traps: *excitado* (with an unfortunate connotation), *realizar* a goal (Spanish *alcanza* or *cumple* it), *suceso* (an event), *actualmente* (currently), the comma in *5,000*, *eventualmente* (possibly), *aplicar para*, *sensibles*, *atender* (to serve), *en el lunes*, *soporte*. In Spain, the warm opening might be *Me hace mucha ilusión anunciar…*, and the team would be addressed as *vosotros*: *sed prudentes*, *vuestro apoyo*.

### Spanish → English

> **Source:** Mi compañera de piso está constipada y no ha podido ir a la librería a recoger los libros que encargó. Es muy sensible y se ha molestado porque nadie la ha ayudado. Actualmente trabaja en una editorial y ha realizado varias traducciones con mucho éxito.

> **Bad translation:** My flatmate is **constipated** and couldn't go to the **library** to collect the books she ordered. She's very **sensible** and got **molested** because nobody helped her. **Actually** she works in an **editorial** and has **realized** several translations with a lot of **exit**.

> **Good translation:** My flatmate has a cold and couldn't go to the bookshop to pick up the books she'd ordered. She's very sensitive and got upset because nobody helped her. She currently works at a publishing house and has done several very successful translations.

Notice that the good translations also change **structure**: *con mucho éxito* becomes an adjective (*very successful*), and *Me alegra muchísimo* replaces a word-for-word *I'm really excited*. Translating well means rewriting.

</Section>

<Section id="anglicisms-culture" kind="culture" title="Anglicisms, the academies and the profession">

Spanish has always borrowed — from Arabic (*almohada*, *aceite*), from French (*jardín*), from Italian (*soneto*), from Nahuatl (*chocolate*) — and English is simply today's main lender. The academies are not purist: their policy is to accept loans that fill a gap, adapt their spelling, and recommend Spanish alternatives when they exist.

- **Adapted** loans are written as Spanish: *fútbol*, *líder*, *estrés*, *eslogan*, *champú*, *esmoquin*.
- **Unadapted** loans keep their spelling and, in careful writing, go in *italics*: *software*, *marketing*, *e-mail* (or the Spanish *correo electrónico*).
- **Regional differences** are large: Latin America says *computadora* (from English), Spain *ordenador* (from French); Mexico *carro*, Spain *coche*; US Spanish borrows the most (see <LessonLink slug="dialects-and-society">Language and society</LessonLink>).
- **Pseudo-anglicisms** are Spanish inventions with English looks: in Spain *el footing* (jogging), *el puenting* (bungee jumping), *el parking* (car park). An English speaker will not recognise them.

Translation is a profession with legal standing in the Spanish-speaking world. Official documents often need a sworn translator — *traductor-intérprete jurado* in Spain, *traductor público* in Argentina, *perito traductor* in Mexico — and Spanish is one of the six official languages of the United Nations.

</Section>

<Section id="misconceptions" kind="misconceptions" title="Common misconceptions">

<Misconception>

**"A false friend is always false."** Many are partial: *discutir* can mean "discuss" as well as "argue"; *asistir* means "to help" in formal and medical use (*asistir a un herido*); *atender* is "to attend to" (*atender a un cliente*). Learn the *other* senses, not a ban.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"Every anglicism is an error."** *Fútbol*, *líder* and *estrés* are Spanish now. The problem is the unnecessary calque that displaces an existing Spanish expression (*hacer sentido* for *tener sentido*), not borrowing as such.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"The gerund is the Spanish -ing."** It covers only the adverbial uses. For *-ing* as a noun Spanish uses the infinitive; as an adjective, a relative clause.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"Machine translation solves this."** Modern tools handle many false friends correctly, but they still reproduce English structure, prepositions and register, and they cannot know whether your reader is in Madrid or Montevideo. Use them as a draft, then edit with this lesson's checklist.

</Misconception>

</Section>
