<Section id="intro" kind="motivation" title="Where you live, and how it compares">

"Is it bigger than your old place?" "Is it as noisy as the centre?" "What's the best café in the neighbourhood?" When people talk about homes and towns, they compare. This last lesson of level A2 gives you the tools for that, plus a form for describing what's going on right now, as on a video call: *I'm unpacking, my neighbour is singing*. We finish with a first look at **por** and **para**, two small words that both often translate as "for", and that you'll keep refining for years.

</Section>

<Section id="comparisons" kind="grammar" title="Comparing: more, less, as … as">

### More and less

**más** / **menos** + adjective, adverb or noun + **que**:

- <Say lang="es">Mi piso nuevo es más grande que el viejo.</Say> My new flat is bigger than the old one.
- <Say lang="es">El centro es más ruidoso que las afueras.</Say>
- <Say lang="es">Este barrio es menos caro que el mío.</Say> This neighbourhood is less expensive than mine.
- <Say lang="es">Mi hermana cocina más rápido que yo.</Say> My sister cooks faster than me.
- <Say lang="es">Aquí hay más parques que en mi ciudad.</Say>

The adjective still agrees with its noun: *La cocina es más **pequeña** que el salón.* After *que*, use the subject pronouns: *más alto que **yo***, *que **tú***.

With **numbers**, use **de** instead of *que*: <Say lang="es">El edificio tiene más de cien años.</Say> (The building is more than a hundred years old.)

### Equal

- **tan** + adjective or adverb + **como**: <Say lang="es">La cocina es tan pequeña como la otra.</Say> (as small as)
- **tanto, tanta, tantos, tantas** + noun + **como**, agreeing with the noun: <Say lang="es">No tengo tantos muebles como tú.</Say> (as much / as many … as) <Say lang="es">Aquí no hay tanta luz como en mi casa.</Say>
- verb + **tanto como**: <Say lang="es">Mi hermano no estudia tanto como yo.</Say> (as much as)

### Four irregular comparatives

| | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| bueno, bien | <Say lang="es">mejor</Say> | better |
| malo, mal | <Say lang="es">peor</Say> | worse |
| (of people's age) | <Say lang="es">mayor</Say> | older |
| (of people's age) | <Say lang="es">menor</Say> | younger |

- <Say lang="es">Este café es mejor que el de la esquina.</Say> (never *más bueno* for quality)
- <Say lang="es">Hoy me siento peor que ayer.</Say>
- <Say lang="es">Mi hermana es mayor que yo.</Say> You already met *mi hermano mayor* in lesson 4.

*Mejor* and *peor* have no feminine form; the plural adds *-es*: *los mejores restaurantes*. For physical size, use *más grande* and *más pequeño*: *Mi casa es más grande que la tuya.*

</Section>

<Section id="superlatives" kind="grammar" title="The most, and -ísimo">

### The biggest, the best

To single something out from a group: **el, la, los, las** (+ noun) + **más / menos** + adjective + **de**:

- <Say lang="es">Es el edificio más alto de la ciudad.</Say> It's the tallest building in the city.
- <Say lang="es">Es la habitación más luminosa de la casa.</Say>
- <Say lang="es">Son los vecinos menos ruidosos del edificio.</Say> (*de* + *el* = *del*)

Notice **de**, where English says "in": *de la ciudad*, not *en la ciudad*. With the irregular forms: <Say lang="es">el mejor restaurante del barrio</Say> (the best restaurant in the neighbourhood), <Say lang="es">la peor habitación</Say>, <Say lang="es">el mayor de mis hermanos</Say> (the eldest of my siblings). *Mejor* and *peor* usually go **before** the noun.

### -ísimo: extremely

To say "very, extremely" without comparing, add **-ísimo** (agreeing: *-ísima, -ísimos, -ísimas*). Drop a final vowel first:

| Adjective | -ísimo |
| --- | --- |
| grande | <Say lang="es">grandísimo</Say> |
| caro | <Say lang="es">carísimo</Say> |
| fácil | <Say lang="es">facilísimo</Say> |
| mucho | <Say lang="es">muchísimo</Say> |

Spelling changes keep the sound, as in the preterite: **rico → riquísimo** (*c → qu*), **largo → larguísimo** (*g → gu*), **feliz → felicísimo** (*z → c*). <Say lang="es">La sopa está riquísima.</Say> (The soup is delicious.) <Say lang="es">Es un piso carísimo.</Say>

The accent always falls on the *í* of the ending, and the original accent disappears: *fácil → facilísimo*.

</Section>

<Section id="progressive" kind="grammar" title="Right now: estar + gerund">

To stress that something is **in progress at this moment**, use **estar** + the **gerund** (the *-ing* form):

| Infinitive | Gerund |
| --- | --- |
| -ar: hablar, cocinar | habl**ando**, cocin**ando** |
| -er: comer, hacer | com**iendo**, hac**iendo** |
| -ir: vivir, escribir | viv**iendo**, escrib**iendo** |

- <Say lang="es">¿Qué estás haciendo?</Say> What are you doing (right now)?
- <Say lang="es">Estoy cocinando.</Say> I'm cooking.
- <Say lang="es">Los niños están jugando en el jardín.</Say>

Irregular gerunds follow patterns you know:

- a vowel before the ending turns *-iendo* into **-yendo**: <Say lang="es">leyendo</Say> (*leer*), <Say lang="es">oyendo</Say> (*oír*), and **yendo** (*ir*)
- -ir stem-changers change as in the preterite (*e → i, o → u*): <Say lang="es">pidiendo</Say>, <Say lang="es">diciendo</Say>, <Say lang="es">durmiendo</Say>, <Say lang="es">sintiendo</Say>

Pronouns go before *estar* or attach to the gerund, which then needs an accent: <Say lang="es">Me estoy duchando.</Say> = <Say lang="es">Estoy duchándome.</Say>

**Use it less than English does.** The simple present already covers "I'm doing" in most contexts (lesson 5): *¿Qué haces?* is as natural as *¿Qué estás haciendo?* And Spanish **never** uses the progressive for future plans: "I'm leaving tomorrow" is *Me voy mañana* or *Voy a salir mañana*, never *estoy saliendo mañana*.

</Section>

<Section id="por-para" kind="grammar" title="Por and para: a first look">

Both often translate as "for", but they divide the work. A helpful image: **para** points **forward**, to a goal or a destination; **por** looks at the **cause**, the **route** or the **exchange**.

| para | por |
| --- | --- |
| **purpose**, "in order to": <Say lang="es">Ahorro para comprar un piso.</Say> I'm saving to buy a flat. | **cause, reason**: <Say lang="es">No salimos por la lluvia.</Say> We didn't go out because of the rain. |
| **recipient**: <Say lang="es">Este regalo es para ti.</Say> This present is for you. | **through, along, around**: <Say lang="es">Paseamos por el barrio.</Say> We walked around the neighbourhood. |
| **destination**: <Say lang="es">Salgo para Lima mañana.</Say> I'm leaving for Lima tomorrow. | **exchange, price**: <Say lang="es">Lo compré por cien euros.</Say> I bought it for a hundred euros. |
| **deadline**: <Say lang="es">Lo necesito para el lunes.</Say> I need it by Monday. | **means**: <Say lang="es">Hablamos por teléfono.</Say> We talked on the phone. |
| **opinion**: <Say lang="es">Para mí, es el mejor barrio.</Say> For me, it's the best area. | **gratitude**: <Say lang="es">Gracias por todo.</Say> Thanks for everything. |

You've used several of these already: *por la mañana*, *pasear por la playa*, *para mí, un café*, *para llevar*. Note *ti* after prepositions: *para ti, por ti* (no accent), but *para mí* (accent). Por and para come back at level B1 and beyond; for now, learn the examples in the table as models.

</Section>

<Section id="dialogue" kind="dialogue" title="A new flat in Mexico City">

Remember Ximena and Priya, who met at a language exchange in Mexico City in lesson 2? Priya is back in Delhi now. Ximena has just moved and calls her on video.

| | Spanish | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Priya | <Say lang="es">¡Hola, Ximena! ¿Qué estás haciendo?</Say> | Hi, Ximena! What are you doing? |
| Ximena | <Say lang="es">¡Hola! Estoy desempacando cajas. Ayer me mudé a un departamento nuevo.</Say> | Hi! I'm unpacking boxes. Yesterday I moved into a new flat. |
| Priya | <Say lang="es">¡Qué emoción! ¿Cómo es?</Say> | How exciting! What's it like? |
| Ximena | <Say lang="es">Mira, te lo muestro. Esta es la sala. Es mucho más grande que la del otro departamento.</Say> | Look, I'll show you. This is the living room. It's much bigger than the one in the other flat. |
| Priya | <Say lang="es">¡Y tiene muchísima luz!</Say> | And it's so bright! |
| Ximena | <Say lang="es">Sí, es el departamento más luminoso del edificio. Está en el sexto piso.</Say> | Yes, it's the brightest flat in the building. It's on the sixth floor. |
| Priya | <Say lang="es">¿Y la cocina?</Say> | And the kitchen? |
| Ximena | <Say lang="es">Es tan pequeña como la otra, pero tiene más espacio para guardar cosas.</Say> | It's as small as the other one, but it has more space to store things. |
| Priya | <Say lang="es">¿Y el barrio, qué tal?</Say> | And how's the neighbourhood? |
| Ximena | <Say lang="es">Es menos ruidoso que el centro y está más cerca de mi trabajo. Ahora tardo veinte minutos, no una hora.</Say> | It's less noisy than the centre and closer to my work. Now it takes me twenty minutes, not an hour. |
| Priya | <Say lang="es">¿Y la renta?</Say> | And the rent? |
| Ximena | <Say lang="es">Es un poco más cara, pero vale la pena.</Say> | It's a bit more expensive, but it's worth it. |
| Priya | <Say lang="es">Oye, ¿quién está cantando?</Say> | Hey, who's singing? |
| Ximena | <Say lang="es">Es mi vecino. Está practicando para un concierto. Canta mejor que yo, ¡pero no tan bien como tú!</Say> | It's my neighbour. He's practising for a concert. He sings better than me, but not as well as you! |
| Priya | <Say lang="es">¡Ja, ja! Mándame fotos de todo, por favor.</Say> | Ha ha! Send me photos of everything, please. |
| Ximena | <Say lang="es">Te las mando esta noche. Y tengo un sofá cama para tus visitas. ¿Cuándo vienes?</Say> | I'll send them to you tonight. And I've got a sofa bed for your visits. When are you coming? |
| Priya | <Say lang="es">¡Por Navidad, si puedo!</Say> | Around Christmas, if I can! |

**Notes.** *La del otro departamento* = "the one of the other flat": *la* stands for *la sala*, so the noun isn't repeated. *Tardar* = to take (time). *Vale la pena* = it's worth it. *Desempacar* is "to unpack" in Latin America (*deshacer las maletas* or *desembalar* in Spain). *Por Navidad* uses *por* for an approximate time: "around Christmas". *Para tus visitas*: *para* for purpose. And *te las mando*: *te* + *las* (the photos), from lesson 10.

</Section>

<Section id="pronunciation" kind="pronunciation" title="Stress in long words">

Many forms in this lesson are long, and the stress falls in a fixed place you can rely on:

- **gerunds** are always stressed on *-AN-* or *-IEN-*: <Say lang="es">cocinando</Say> (co-ci-NAN-do), <Say lang="es">escribiendo</Say> (es-cri-BIEN-do), <Say lang="es">leyendo</Say> (le-YEN-do)
- **-ísimo** is always stressed on the *í*, wherever the original stress was: <Say lang="es">grande</Say> (GRAN-de) → <Say lang="es">grandísimo</Say> (gran-DÍ-si-mo); <Say lang="es">fácil</Say> → <Say lang="es">facilísimo</Say>
- adding a pronoun to a gerund keeps the stress, so an accent appears: <Say lang="es">duchándome</Say>, <Say lang="es">leyéndolo</Say>

Also practise the linking of *más* and *menos* with the following word: <Say lang="es">más alto</Say> sounds like "ma-SAL-to", <Say lang="es">menos ocupado</Say> like "me-no-so-cu-PA-do".

</Section>

<Section id="culture" kind="culture" title="Pisos, porteros and which floor is the first">

In Spanish cities, most people live in flats rather than houses, often in blocks around an inner courtyard, and many buildings have a *portero* (doorman or caretaker) or an intercom (*el portero automático* in Spain, *el interfón* in Mexico). In many Latin American cities, gated residential complexes and houses with walled patios are also common, and in older parts of southern Spain you'll still find houses built around a central *patio* full of plants.

Floors are numbered differently from place to place. In Spain, Mexico and Argentina, the ground floor is *la planta baja* and *el primer piso* is the one above it, as in Britain. In some other countries, such as Colombia, *el primer piso* is the ground floor, as in the United States. When in doubt, look at the lift buttons. And Spanish addresses put the floor and door after the street number: *Calle Mayor 12, 3.º B* is the flat on the third floor, door B.

When you arrive at someone's home, you may hear <Say lang="es">Estás en tu casa</Say> or, more formally, <Say lang="es">Está en su casa</Say>: "make yourself at home", literally "you're in your house".

</Section>

<Section id="misconceptions" kind="misconceptions" title="Common mix-ups">

<Misconception>

"*Más mejor*, *más peor*." *Mejor* and *peor* already mean "more good" and "more bad": **Este café es mejor**.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

"The biggest room *in* the house" is *la habitación más grande en la casa*. Spanish uses *de* in superlatives: **la habitación más grande de la casa**.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

"*Estoy saliendo mañana*, like English 'I'm leaving tomorrow'." Spanish doesn't use the progressive for the future: **Salgo mañana** or **Voy a salir mañana**.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

"*Tan* and *tanto* are interchangeable." *Tan* goes with adjectives and adverbs (*tan grande como*); *tanto* goes with nouns, and agrees with them (*tantas ventanas como*), or after a verb (*trabaja tanto como yo*).

</Misconception>

</Section>
