<Section id="accent-rules" kind="grammar" title="The written accent: the complete rules">

Every Spanish word of more than one syllable has one stressed syllable. The written accent tells the reader where it is whenever the general pattern does not.

### The general rules

| Type | Takes an accent when it ends in… | Examples | Without accent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Aguda** | a vowel, *-n* or *-s* | *café, canción, compás* | *reloj, papel, feliz*; also *-y* (*virrey*) and consonant + *s* (*robots*) |
| **Llana** | anything **except** a vowel, *-n* or *-s* | *árbol, lápiz, fácil, cáncer*; also consonant + *s* (*bíceps*) and *-y* (*póney*) | *casa, examen, lunes* |
| **Esdrújula** and **sobresdrújula** | always | *música, rápido, dígamelo* | — |

**Monosyllables** never take an accent, except for the diacritical pairs below: *fue, fui, dio, vio, pie, mes, sol*. Since the 2010 *Ortografía*, words such as *guion*, *truhan*, *fie*, *lie* and *rio* (from *reír*) count as monosyllables for spelling purposes and are written **without** an accent.

### Diphthongs and hiatus

- A **diphthong** (an unstressed *i* or *u* next to *a*, *e*, *o*, or *iu / ui*) counts as one syllable, and the word follows the general rules: *acción* (aguda in *-n*), *náutico* (esdrújula), *huésped* (llana in *-d*), *construí* (aguda in a vowel). When the accent falls on a diphthong, it goes on the *a, e, o*; in *iu* and *ui*, on the second vowel: *cuídate*, *lingüístico*.
- A **stressed *i* or *u* next to *a, e, o*** breaks the diphthong into a **hiatus**, and it **always** takes an accent, whatever the general rules say: *día, país, baúl, reír, oído, frío, Raúl*. The *h* makes no difference: *búho*, *prohíbe*.
- Two strong vowels in hiatus follow the general rules: *po-e-ta*, *le-ón*, *ca-ó-ti-co*.

### Compounds and attached pronouns

- Compound words follow the general rules as one word: *decimoséptimo*, *asimismo* (no accent: llana in a vowel), *tiovivo*.
- Adverbs in *-mente* keep the adjective's accent, if it had one: *fácilmente*, *cortésmente*, *rápidamente* — but *lentamente*.
- Verbs with attached pronouns follow the general rules: *deme* and *estate* (llanas in a vowel: no accent), *dámelo*, *mírame*, *pidiéndole* (esdrújulas).
- **Capitals carry accents**: *Ángel*, *ÉXITO*, *Álvarez*. The old idea that they don't comes from typewriters.

</Section>

<Section id="diacritics" kind="grammar" title="The diacritical accent, solo and the demonstratives">

The **tilde diacrítica** distinguishes words with the same spelling but different functions — mostly monosyllables.

| With accent | Without |
| --- | --- |
| *tú* (you) | *tu* (your) |
| *él* (he) | *el* (the) |
| *mí* (me) | *mi* (my; the note E) |
| *sí* (yes; himself/herself) | *si* (if; the note B) |
| *té* (tea) | *te* (you, object pronoun) |
| *dé* (give, from *dar*) | *de* (of, from) |
| *sé* (I know; be!) | *se* (pronoun) |
| *más* (more) | *mas* (but, literary) |
| *aún* (still, yet = *todavía*) | *aun* (even = *incluso*) |
| *qué, quién, cuál, cómo, cuándo, dónde, cuánto* — in questions and exclamations, **direct or indirect** | *que, quien, cual, como, cuando, donde, cuanto* — relatives and conjunctions |

The indirect questions are where most errors happen: *No sé **qué** hacer.* *Pregúntale **dónde** vive.* *Depende de **cuánto** cueste.* But: *Lo hice **como** me dijiste* (manner, not a question).

The *porque* family:

- ***por qué*** — why: *¿Por qué no vienes?* · *No sé por qué.*
- ***porque*** — because: *No voy porque llueve.*
- ***el porqué*** — the reason (a noun): *Explícame el porqué.*
- ***por que*** — preposition + relative: *la razón por (la) que dimití*.

Since 2010, *o* between numbers no longer takes an accent: *3 o 4*.

### *Solo* and the demonstratives

For decades Spanish wrote *sólo* (only) to distinguish it from *solo* (alone), and *éste, ése, aquél* as pronouns to distinguish them from the determiners *este, ese, aquel*. The 2010 *Ortografía* established that these words, being regular *llanas* ending in a vowel, are written **without** an accent. The Academy clarified its position in 2023: where there is no risk of ambiguity the accent must not be written; where the writer judges that a sentence could be read in two ways, the accent is **optional**.

> *Trabajo solo los domingos.* — "I only work on Sundays" or "On Sundays I work alone"?

The best solution is usually to rewrite: *Solamente trabajo los domingos* / *Los domingos trabajo sin compañía*. Note that *está* (is) always has an accent — it is an *aguda* in a vowel — and has nothing to do with the demonstrative *esta*.

</Section>

<Section id="punctuation" kind="grammar" title="Punctuation: where Spanish differs">

Most punctuation works as in English. These are the differences that mark a careful writer.

- **Opening signs** go where the question or exclamation begins, not necessarily at the start of the sentence: *Si llueve, **¿**vendrás?* · *Pues **¡**qué sorpresa!*
- **No comma between subject and verb**, however long the subject: *Los alumnos que no hayan entregado el trabajo antes del viernes **deberán** hablar con la profesora* — no comma before *deberán*.
- **Vocatives always take commas**: *Hola, Marta.* · *Gracias, profesor.* · *Marta, ven.* This is the commonest comma error in emails.
- **Before *pero*, *sino*, *aunque***, a comma: *Es caro, pero merece la pena.* **No serial comma** before *y* in a simple list: *pan, queso y vino*.
- **Connectors** at the start of a sentence are followed by a comma (*Sin embargo, …*), and in the middle are enclosed by commas.
- **Colon after a letter's greeting**, with a capital on the next line: *Estimada Sra. López:* — not a comma, as in English.
- **Quotation marks**: the recommended order is « », then “ ”, then ‘ ’. The full stop goes **after** the closing mark: *Dijo: «No vendré».*
- **The dash** (*raya*, —) introduces dialogue and the narrator's comments: *—¿Vienes? —preguntó Ana.* It is not the hyphen (*guion*, -).
- **Ellipsis**: exactly three dots, and never together with *etc.*
- **Capitals** are much rarer than in English: days, months, languages, nationalities and religions are lower case (*el lunes 4 de marzo*, *el inglés*, *una escritora chilena*), and so are titles and offices in general use (*el rey*, *el papa*, *la ministra de Educación* — the ministry itself keeps its capital). Titles of works capitalise only the first word: *El amor en los tiempos del cólera*.

</Section>

<Section id="classic-errors" kind="grammar" title="The classic errors">

### Dequeísmo and queísmo

**Dequeísmo** adds a *de* the verb does not take: *Pienso **de** que tienes razón* → *Pienso que tienes razón.* **Queísmo** drops a *de* the verb needs: *Me acuerdo que…* → *Me acuerdo **de** que…*

The test: replace the *que*-clause with *eso*.

- *Pienso **eso*** (not *pienso de eso*) → *Pienso que…*
- *Me acuerdo **de eso*** → *Me acuerdo de que…*
- *Estoy seguro **de eso*** → *Estoy seguro de que…*
- *Me di cuenta **de eso*** → *Me di cuenta de que…*
- *Me alegro **de eso*** → *Me alegro de que…*
- *Es posible **eso*** → *Es posible que…*

Queísmo is very widespread in speech, especially in Latin America (*darse cuenta que*), and dequeísmo is socially stigmatised; the norm rejects both.

### Leísmo, laísmo, loísmo

The norm assigns *lo / la / los / las* to direct objects and *le / les* to indirect objects. Central Spain often blurs the line:

| Phenomenon | Example | Norm's view |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Leísmo** with a male person | *A Juan **le** vi ayer.* | Accepted, because it is so widespread among educated speakers in Spain; *lo vi* is also correct (and the only form in most of Latin America). |
| Leísmo with things or women | *Este libro **le** compré.* · *A María **le** vi.* | Not accepted: *lo compré*, *la vi*. The plural *les* for men (*A tus hermanos les vi*) is best avoided. |
| **Laísmo** | *A María **la** dije la verdad.* | Not accepted: *le dije*. |
| **Loísmo** | *A Pedro **lo** di un regalo.* | Not accepted and strongly stigmatised: *le di*. |

### Agreement

- **Collective nouns** in the singular take a singular verb: *La gente **dice**…* (not *dicen*); *El equipo **ganó**.*
- **Collective + *de* + plural** allows both: *La mayoría de los asistentes **votó / votaron** a favor.* With a singular complement, singular only: *La mayoría **del equipo** trabajó.*
- **Existential *haber*** is impersonal and stays singular: ***Había** muchas personas* (not *habían*); ***Hubo** problemas* (not *hubieron*).

### Confusable pairs

*sino* (but rather) / *si no* (if not): *No es rojo, **sino** azul.* · ***Si no** vienes, me voy.* — *a ver* (let's see) / *haber* (to have; there to be) — *hay* (there is) / *ahí* (there) / *ay* (ouch) — *hecho* (done, fact) / *echo* (I throw) — *vaya* (go; well!) / *valla* (fence) / *baya* (berry).

</Section>

>

>

<Section id="editing" kind="culture" title="Editing your own writing">

Professional writers in Spanish edit in passes, each looking for one kind of problem. A checklist for your own texts:

1. **Read aloud.** Your ear will catch broken agreement, missing words and sentences that run out of breath.
2. **Accents, word by word.** Check every verb in the preterite and future (*habló, hablará*), every *esta / está*, *el / él*, *tu / tú*, *mas / más*, and every question word, direct or indirect.
3. **Search for *que*.** For each *de que*, apply the *eso* test; for each verb + *que*, ask whether the verb needs *de*.
4. **Search for *le* and *la*.** Is the object direct or indirect?
5. **Commas.** None between subject and verb; always around vocatives; before *pero*; after fronted connectors and conditions.
6. **Agreement.** Collective nouns, *haber*, long subjects whose verb is far away.
7. **Style.** Cut pleonasms and empty support verbs (see <LessonLink slug="style-and-register">Style and register</LessonLink>); check gerunds and false friends (see <LessonLink slug="translation-pitfalls">Translation pitfalls</LessonLink>).
8. **Format.** Dates (*3 de mayo de 2026*), numbers (*5000*, *12 345*), capitals, quotation marks.

### The tilde as a public passion

Spelling rules are front-page news in the Spanish-speaking world. When the 2010 *Ortografía* removed the accent from *guion* and recommended dropping it from *solo*, newspapers ran columns for months; some well-known writers, including members of the Academy, announced that they would go on writing *sólo*. The 2023 clarification was again national news. Behind the passion is a shared conviction: in Spanish, the accent is not decoration but part of the word.

<Reflection title="Faithful in little">

"He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much" (Luke 16:10). A missing accent or a stray comma is a small thing, and no one is a worse person for it. But careful writing is a form of attention to the reader — a way of saying that their time and understanding matter. Checking an email once more before sending it is a small courtesy, and small courtesies are where larger ones are learned.

</Reflection>

<Section id="misconceptions" kind="misconceptions" title="Common misconceptions">

<Misconception>

**"Capitals don't take accents."** They do, and always have; typewriters and early computers simply made it hard. *ÁFRICA*, *Ángela*, *ÚLTIMA HORA*.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"Accents are optional in informal writing."** Readers can often guess, but not always: *papa* (potato; the Pope) / *papá* (dad); *publico* (I publish) / *público* (public) / *publicó* (published); *esta* (this) / *está* (is).

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"*Sólo* is now always wrong."** The current rule: no accent where there is no ambiguity (and there almost never is); optional where the writer judges there is. In practice, write *solo*, and rewrite the rare ambiguous sentence.

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"Spanish puts commas where English does."** Not between a long subject and its verb, not before the *y* of a simple list, and always around vocatives — three places where English habits mislead.

</Misconception>

</Section>
