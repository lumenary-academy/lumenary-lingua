<Section id="vocabulary" kind="vocabulary" title="Words for work">

This lesson brings together two things that go well with a job search: the **conditional**, the tense of "would", which makes requests polite and lets you imagine; and **por** and **para**, which you met briefly at A2 and which turn up in every other sentence about work (*trabajo para…, gracias por…, para el lunes*).

<Section id="conditional" kind="grammar" title="The conditional">

### Formation

The conditional is one of the easiest tenses to build. Take the **whole infinitive** and add the endings **-ía, -ías, -ía, -íamos, -íais, -ían**. The endings are the same for all three conjugations.

| | hablar | comer | vivir |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| yo | hablaría | comería | viviría |
| tú | hablarías | comerías | vivirías |
| él, ella, usted | hablaría | comería | viviría |
| nosotros, nosotras | hablaríamos | comeríamos | viviríamos |
| vosotros, vosotras | hablaríais | comeríais | viviríais |
| ellos, ellas, ustedes | hablarían | comerían | vivirían |

The irregular verbs are exactly the ones that are irregular in the future (see <LessonLink slug="plans-and-future">Plans and future</LessonLink>), with the same stems:

| Infinitive | Stem | Conditional (yo) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| tener, poner, salir, venir | tendr-, pondr-, saldr-, vendr- | tendría, pondría, saldría, vendría |
| poder, saber, haber, querer | podr-, sabr-, habr-, querr- | podría, sabría, habría, querría |
| hacer, decir | har-, dir- | haría, diría |

*Hay* becomes *habría* (there would be).

### Uses

**1. Imagining.** What you would do in a situation that isn't real, or isn't real yet:

- <Say lang="es">Me encantaría trabajar en Chile.</Say> — I'd love to work in Chile.
- <Say lang="es">Con más tiempo, aprendería a tocar la guitarra.</Say> — With more time, I'd learn to play the guitar.
- <Say lang="es">¿Qué harías tú en mi situación?</Say> — What would you do in my situation?

The full "if" sentences (*si tuviera tiempo, aprendería…*) need the imperfect subjunctive, which is the subject of <LessonLink slug="if-i-were">If I were…</LessonLink>.

**2. Polite requests.** The conditional softens a request, much like English *could* and *would*:

| More direct | More polite |
| --- | --- |
| *¿Puede repetirlo?* | <Say lang="es">¿Podría repetirlo, por favor?</Say> |
| *Quiero hablar con la directora.* | <Say lang="es">Me gustaría hablar con la directora.</Say> |
| *¿Tienes un momento?* | <Say lang="es">¿Tendrías un momento?</Say> |
| *Cierre la ventana, por favor.* | <Say lang="es">¿Le importaría cerrar la ventana?</Say> |

**3. Advice.** Three set expressions, all with the conditional:

- <Say lang="es">Deberías pedir un aumento.</Say> — You should ask for a raise.
- <Say lang="es">Yo que tú, aceptaría la oferta.</Say> — If I were you, I'd accept the offer.
- <Say lang="es">Yo en tu lugar, no diría nada todavía.</Say> — In your place, I wouldn't say anything yet.

**4. The future seen from the past.** <Say lang="es">Dijo que llamaría el lunes.</Say> — She said she would call on Monday. You will use this a lot in <LessonLink slug="reported-speech">Reported speech</LessonLink>.

**5. A guess about the past.** <Say lang="es">Serían las diez cuando llegó.</Say> — It must have been about ten when he arrived.

</Section>

<Section id="por-and-para" kind="grammar" title="Por and para">

Both can translate English *for*, which is why they cause trouble. A useful picture: **para** looks ahead, to a goal, a destination, a recipient or a deadline; **por** looks at a cause behind you, a route you pass through, or something exchanged.

### Para

| Use | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose (+ infinitive: *in order to*) | <Say lang="es">Estudio español para trabajar en México.</Say> |
| Destination | <Say lang="es">Mañana salgo para Lima.</Say> |
| Recipient | <Say lang="es">Este informe es para la jefa.</Say> |
| Deadline | <Say lang="es">Lo necesito para el viernes.</Say> |
| Opinion | <Say lang="es">Para mí, es el mejor puesto.</Say> |
| Comparison with what's expected | <Say lang="es">Para ser verano, hace mucho frío.</Say> |
| Employer | <Say lang="es">Trabajo para una ONG.</Say> |
| What something is for | *una taza para café* (a coffee cup) |

### Por

| Use | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Cause, reason (*because of*) | <Say lang="es">Cerraron la oficina por la nieve.</Say> |
| Thanks and apologies | <Say lang="es">Gracias por su tiempo. Perdón por el retraso.</Say> |
| Through, along, around | <Say lang="es">Paseamos por el centro.</Say> *Por aquí* = around here |
| Means of communication | *por teléfono, por correo, por internet* |
| Exchange, price | <Say lang="es">Lo compré por veinte euros.</Say> *Cambiar una cosa por otra* |
| On behalf of, instead of | <Say lang="es">Hoy trabajo por mi compañera, que está enferma.</Say> |
| Part of the day; approximate time | *por la mañana, por la tarde, por julio* |
| Rate (*per*) | *dos veces por semana, cien kilómetros por hora* |
| Agent of a passive | *escrito por Cervantes* (see <LessonLink slug="science-and-the-impersonal">Science and the impersonal</LessonLink>) |
| Duration (often left out) | *Viví allí (por) dos años.* More common in Latin America |
| Fixed phrases | *por favor, por fin, por eso, por ejemplo, por supuesto, por lo menos, por si acaso* |

<Example title="Same sentence, different meaning">

- *Lo hice **para** ti.* — I made it for you (it's a gift). / *Lo hice **por** ti.* — I did it for your sake, because of you.
- *Trabajo **para** mi hermana.* — My sister is my boss. / *Trabajo **por** mi hermana.* — I'm covering for my sister.
- *Salimos **para** Madrid.* — We're leaving for Madrid. / *Pasamos **por** Madrid.* — We're going through Madrid.
- *Estudia **para** ser médica.* — She's studying to become a doctor (goal). / *Estudia medicina **por** sus padres.* — She studies medicine because of her parents (reason).

</Example>

</Section>

<Section id="dialogue" kind="dialogue" title="A video interview">

**A video call between Mexico City and Lima.** Gabriela Morales, from a publisher of children's books in Mexico City, interviews Daniela Ortega, a graphic designer in Lima. As is usual in an interview, they use *usted*.

| | Spanish | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sra. Morales | <Say lang="es">Buenos días, Daniela. Gracias por su tiempo. ¿Podría hablarme un poco de su experiencia?</Say> | Good morning, Daniela. Thank you for your time. Could you tell me a little about your experience? |
| Daniela | <Say lang="es">Buenos días. Claro. Trabajo como diseñadora gráfica desde hace cinco años, primero para una agencia de publicidad y ahora por mi cuenta.</Say> | Good morning. Of course. I've been working as a graphic designer for five years, first for an advertising agency and now freelance. |
| Sra. Morales | <Say lang="es">¿Y por qué le interesa este puesto?</Say> | And why are you interested in this position? |
| Daniela | <Say lang="es">Porque me encantaría diseñar libros para niños. Para mí, es lo más bonito de esta profesión. Además, me gustaría volver a trabajar en equipo.</Say> | Because I'd love to design children's books. For me, it's the best thing about this profession. And I'd like to work in a team again. |
| Sra. Morales | <Say lang="es">Imagine que tiene que entregar unas ilustraciones para el viernes y no le alcanza el tiempo. ¿Qué haría?</Say> | Imagine you have to deliver some illustrations by Friday and you're running out of time. What would you do? |
| Daniela | <Say lang="es">Hablaría con la persona responsable lo antes posible. Le explicaría el problema y le propondría una solución: por ejemplo, entregar una parte el viernes y el resto el lunes. No esperaría hasta el último día.</Say> | I'd talk to the person in charge as soon as possible. I'd explain the problem and suggest a solution: for example, delivering part of it on Friday and the rest on Monday. I wouldn't wait until the last day. |
| Sra. Morales | <Say lang="es">Me parece muy sensato. El puesto es de tiempo completo. ¿Estaría dispuesta a mudarse a la Ciudad de México?</Say> | That seems very sensible. The position is full-time. Would you be willing to move to Mexico City? |
| Daniela | <Say lang="es">Sí, estaría dispuesta. ¿Podría decirme cuál sería el horario?</Say> | Yes, I would. Could you tell me what the hours would be? |
| Sra. Morales | <Say lang="es">De nueve a seis, de lunes a viernes. Los viernes por la tarde se puede teletrabajar.</Say> | Nine to six, Monday to Friday. On Friday afternoons you can work from home. |
| Daniela | <Say lang="es">Perfecto. Una última pregunta, si me permite: ¿cuándo tendrían una respuesta?</Say> | Perfect. One last question, if I may: when would you have an answer? |
| Sra. Morales | <Say lang="es">Le escribiremos por correo antes del día quince.</Say> | We'll write to you by email before the fifteenth. |

**Later, Daniela calls her friend Marcos.**

| | Spanish | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marcos | <Say lang="es">¿Y? ¿Qué tal te fue?</Say> | Well? How did it go? |
| Daniela | <Say lang="es">Creo que bien. Pero si me lo ofrecen, no sé qué voy a hacer. Extrañaría mucho a mi familia.</Say> | Well, I think. But if they offer it to me, I don't know what I'll do. I'd miss my family a lot. |
| Marcos | <Say lang="es">Yo que tú, aceptaría sin pensarlo. Es el trabajo de tus sueños.</Say> | If I were you, I'd accept without thinking twice. It's your dream job. |
| Daniela | <Say lang="es">Ya, pero Ciudad de México está muy lejos.</Say> | Sure, but Mexico City is a long way away. |
| Marcos | <Say lang="es">Yo en tu lugar, lo probaría por un año. Siempre podrías volver.</Say> | In your place, I'd try it for a year. You could always come back. |
| Daniela | <Say lang="es">Tienes razón. Primero, que me lo ofrezcan.</Say> | You're right. They've got to offer it to me first. |

Look for the conditional doing all its jobs: polite requests (*¿Podría hablarme…?, ¿Podría decirme…?*), imagining (*¿Qué haría? Hablaría…, explicaría…*), advice (*yo que tú, aceptaría; yo en tu lugar, lo probaría*) and the softened *me encantaría, me gustaría*. Then count the *por*s and *para*s, and name the use of each.

<Note>

*Extrañar* is the usual verb for "to miss" someone in Latin America; in Spain you will more often hear *echar de menos*. *Ya* is used in Peru, Chile and elsewhere to mean "OK, sure"; in Spain, *ya* as a reply often means "I know" or "yeah". *No me alcanza el tiempo* (I'm running out of time) is typical of Mexico and much of Latin America; in Spain you'd hear *no me da tiempo*.

</Note>

<Section id="pronunciation" kind="pronunciation" title="Quería or querría?">

The conditional's endings carry a written accent on the **í**, which splits *-ía* into two syllables: <Say lang="es">hablaría</Say> is *ha-bla-RÍ-a*, five syllables. Don't let it collapse into *-rya*.

Then listen for two pairs that differ only in sound:

| Pair | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| <Say lang="es">comería</Say> / <Say lang="es">comerá</Say> | would eat / will eat |
| <Say lang="es">quería</Say> / <Say lang="es">querría</Say> | I wanted / I would want |

The second pair depends on the difference between the tapped **r** (one quick flick of the tongue, as in *pero*) and the trilled **rr** (several flicks, as in *perro*). The conditional of *querer* has the trill: *que-RRÍ-a*. Practise with other pairs where the distinction matters: <Say lang="es">pero</Say> / <Say lang="es">perro</Say> (but / dog), <Say lang="es">caro</Say> / <Say lang="es">carro</Say> (expensive / car). If the trill is hard for you, *quisiera* is a handy alternative to *querría* in polite requests: <Say lang="es">Quisiera hablar con la directora.</Say>

</Section>

<Section id="culture" kind="culture" title="Work across the Spanish-speaking world">

Forms of address at work vary. In many offices in Spain colleagues and even bosses use *tú* with each other from the first day. In many Latin American workplaces *usted* is more common, especially with superiors and clients; in Colombia and Costa Rica some people use *usted* even with family and friends. In an interview, start with *usted*; if the other person switches to *tú*, you can follow.

Professional titles are also used more than in English in some countries. In Mexico you will hear *licenciado* or *licenciada* (for someone with a university degree, often shortened to *Lic.*), *ingeniero*, *arquitecta* or *doctora* used as a form of address: *Buenos días, licenciada Morales.*

Pay differs too. In Mexico, the *aguinaldo*, a year-end bonus, is required by law. In Spain, many salaries are paid in fourteen instalments, with two *pagas extra*, usually in summer and at Christmas. And in Spain, when a public holiday falls on a Tuesday or a Thursday, many people take the Monday or Friday off as well, making a *puente* (bridge).

</Section>

<Section id="misconceptions" kind="misconceptions" title="Common mistakes">

<Misconception>

**"Would" always means the conditional.** When "would" describes a past habit, Spanish uses the imperfect: *When I was a child, I would walk to school* = *De niña, **caminaba** a la escuela.* When it means "refused", it's the preterite of *querer*: *She wouldn't open the door* = ***No quiso** abrir la puerta.*

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"Gracias para tu ayuda."** Thanks go with *por*, because your help is the reason for my thanks: ***Gracias por** tu ayuda.*

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"Yo que tú, iré."** The advice formula needs the conditional: *Yo que tú, **iría**.*

</Misconception>

<Misconception>

**"Para + reason."** *Para* never means "because of": *No fui a trabajar **por** la lluvia*, not *para la lluvia*. If you can say "in order to" or "destined for", it's *para*; if you can say "because of", "through" or "in exchange for", it's *por*.

</Misconception>

</Section>
